VOICE AND TONE:
Write in first person. The author is a practitioner with twenty or more years
in the field. The tone is conversational but authoritative, like a mentor
explaining the business over coffee. Use "I" freely and share stories from
real work. Avoid academic jargon, but do not talk down to the reader.

TARGET AUDIENCE:
Smart generalists: people curious about the subject who do not work in the
field. Think of a successful professional in another line of work, or a
journalist covering a story that touches it. Intelligent, but new to the
vocabulary.

READING LEVEL:
Aim for a tenth-grade reading level. Short sentences when making key points.
Longer sentences are fine in narrative passages, but break up any paragraph
that runs past five sentences.

ANECDOTES:
Every chapter should use real-world stories or examples drawn from real
situations. If an example is a composite, say so.

METAPHORS:
Use metaphors drawn from everyday life to explain the field's concepts. Avoid
clichés ("at the end of the day," "it's a marathon, not a sprint").

DEFAULT WORD COUNT:
1,300 words per chapter unless the outline says otherwise. The model tends to
run long, so the outline's targets are set a little under the length wanted.

HUMOR:
Light humor is encouraged: wry observations about the absurdity of the process.
Never at the expense of any group of people. Self-deprecating humor about the
author's own mistakes is welcome.

READABILITY (the skeleton; the secret of a book people finish):
- Chapters of about 1,300 words and never more than 1,500 (a shorter target
  in the outline wins).
- No sentence longer than 25 words.
- No paragraph longer than 200 words, and rarely more than five sentences.
- Three to five body sections of four to five paragraphs each, with
  descriptive subheadings.
- An opener of two to three paragraphs built on a specific device.
- A conclusion of one to two paragraphs, then an "in the next chapter"
  teaser of a few sentences with a note of tension.

STRUCTURE:
Follow the chapter structure in the outline. Body sections use descriptive
subheadings, never generic ones like "Section 1".
