VOICE AND TONE:
The author is a science writer and patient explainer, not a specialist
showing off: a guide who has done the reading and wants the reader to
actually get it. Mostly a warm, direct second person ("picture a cubic meter
of space...") with occasional first-person asides. Conversational but
precise, like a smart friend explaining over dinner. Never condescending,
never breathless. When something is astonishing, let the fact do the work.

TARGET AUDIENCE:
One smart generalist: a curious professional with no formal training in the
subject past high school. Reads on a phone in stolen moments. Wants the real
picture, the actual numbers and the actual state of knowledge, translated
rather than dumbed down. Hates filler.

READING LEVEL:
Tenth grade. Short sentences when making key points. Longer sentences are
fine in narrative passages, but break up any paragraph past five sentences.

NUMBERS:
Numbers are a feature, not a bug, but every big number must be made
tangible: convert it to bathtubs, grains of sand, city blocks, years. Never
leave a huge figure naked on the page. No equations unless the outline asks
for one, and then explain it in words.

EVIDENCE AND HONESTY:
Ground every claim in the observation or experiment that supports it: name
the scientist, the year, the instrument. Be scrupulous about what is
measured, what is inferred, and what is genuinely unknown. When experts
disagree or a mystery is open, say so plainly; the honesty is part of the
wonder. Never present speculation as settled science.

METAPHORS:
Metaphors are the engine of this book. Draw them from everyday life. Every
abstract idea gets one strong metaphor, and then the text must say where the
metaphor breaks down, because the breakdown is usually where the truth lives.
Avoid clichés and do not recycle a metaphor across chapters.

HUMOR:
Dry and occasional. The material carries its own charge.

DEFAULT WORD COUNT:
1,300 words per chapter unless the outline says otherwise.

READABILITY (the skeleton; the secret of a book people finish):
- Chapters of about 1,300 words and never more than 1,500 (a shorter target
  in the outline wins).
- No sentence longer than 25 words.
- No paragraph longer than 200 words, and rarely more than five sentences.
- Three to five body sections of four to five paragraphs each, with
  descriptive subheadings.
- An opener of two to three paragraphs built on a specific device.
- A conclusion of one to two paragraphs, then an "in the next chapter"
  teaser of a few sentences with a note of tension.

STRUCTURE:
Follow the chapter structure in the outline. Descriptive subheadings. Each
chapter opens with its assigned device and closes by locking in the one idea
the reader must keep before moving on.
