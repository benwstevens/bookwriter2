## 1. Historical anchors with dates, tolls, and turning points

The recurring template across 2,500 years is Thucydidean: symptom catalog → moral and social collapse → scapegoating → eventual adaptation. The same pattern recurs in Procopius, Boccaccio, Defoe, Camus, and 2020 New York Times obituaries. Two natural experiments anchor the whole book: **Philadelphia vs. St. Louis 1918** (the timing of NPIs) and **Eyam 1666** (the moral economy of voluntary containment). Both pre-date germ theory; both still teach the modern lesson.

### Plague of Athens (430–426 BC)
~75,000–100,000 dead (25–33% of Athens). DNA from a 1994 Kerameikos mass grave (Papagrigorakis et al., 2006) suggests **typhoid fever** (Salmonella enterica Typhi). **Pivotal because** Pericles's decision to crowd the Attic countryside behind the Long Walls turned a military strategy into a public-health catastrophe; the plague killed Pericles in 429 BC and tipped Athens toward the demagogues and the Sicilian disaster. Thucydides — infected and recovered — wrote the first epidemiological narrative in Western literature and the first record of acquired immunity. **Vivid detail:** citizens dying around the public fountains they crawled toward; "men became careless of the law… seeing how quick was the change of fortune."

### Antonine Plague (165–180 AD)
~5–10 million dead; ~7–10% of the Roman Empire; up to 2,000 deaths/day in Rome. Likely smallpox (Galen described black exanthem and pustules). Marcus Aurelius probably died of it. **Pivotal because** Kyle Harper (*The Fate of Rome*) treats this as the inflection point that begins Rome's long demographic descent; Marcus Aurelius had to conscript slaves and gladiators to fill the legions. **Vivid detail:** Galen, the empire's most prestigious physician, fled Rome in 166 — preserving his medical writings while exposing the limits of ancient medicine.

### Plague of Justinian (541–549 AD; recurrences to ~750)
~25–50 million dead in the first wave; Procopius reports 10,000 deaths/day at peak in Constantinople (modern estimates: 5,000). First documented *Yersinia pestis* pandemic, confirmed by ancient DNA (Aschheim, Jerash). **Pivotal because** it aborted Justinian's reconquest of the western Mediterranean under Belisarius — arguably one of history's most consequential what-ifs. **Vivid detail:** mass pits at Sycae across the Golden Horn, bodies piled "like cargo in a ship."

### Black Death and the birth of quarantine (1347–1351; quarantine law 1377)
~25–50 million dead in Europe (30–50% of population); globally 75–200 million. *Y. pestis* via the Silk Road; entered Sicily October 1347 via Genoese galleys from Caffa. **Pivotal because** on **July 27, 1377**, the Great Council of Ragusa (Dubrovnik) issued the **first quarantine law** — a 30-day *trentino* on the islets of Mrkan/Bobara. Venice extended it to 40 days (*quaranta giorni*), opened the first permanent lazaretto (Santa Maria di Nazareth, 1423), and Ragusa appointed the first plague officials (*kacamorti*) in 1426 and kept contact-tracing ledgers by 1457. **Public health invented without germ theory.** Simultaneously, ~350 anti-Jewish pogroms killed thousands; **Strasbourg, Feb 14, 1349**, burned ~2,000 Jews alive *before plague had reached the city* — debt cancellation and property seizure hiding behind a poisoning conspiracy that Pope Clement VI's bulls (*Sicut Judeis*, July 1348; *Quamvis Perfidiam*, September 1348) explicitly condemned but could not stop.

### Columbian Exchange / smallpox in the Americas (1492 onward)
Indigenous population fell ~80–95% over a century — perhaps 60M → 5–6M by 1600. First smallpox outbreak on Hispaniola Dec 1518; reached Tenochtitlán 1520 (killing Cuitláhuac); reached the Inca by ~1524 (killing Huayna Capac, triggering the civil war that left Atahualpa vulnerable to Pizarro). **Pivotal because** without smallpox neither Cortés (~1,000 men) nor Pizarro (168 at Cajamarca) could have toppled empires of millions. **Vivid detail:** the Florentine Codex — "the pustules covered them on the face, the head, the chest… there was death from hunger." This is also the master case of **biological warfare intent**: the Amherst–Bouquet correspondence (July 1763) explicitly proposing smallpox-blanket transfer to Lenape envoys at Fort Pitt — documented intent unambiguous, actual epidemiological contribution debated.

### Great Plague of London and Eyam (1665–1666)
London: ~100,000 dead (25% of population); peak week September 1665 = 7,165 burials. Ended with the **Great Fire (Sept 2–6, 1666)**. Eyam, Derbyshire: of ~350 villagers, ~260 died. **Pivotal because** Rector William Mompesson and the ejected Puritan Thomas Stanley persuaded villagers in spring 1666 to seal themselves off to protect Sheffield and the north — the first **voluntary cordon sanitaire**. Modeling (Whittles & Didelot, 2016) suggests the quarantine probably *raised* Eyam's own death toll by concentrating pneumonic transmission — the first documented trade-off between local and aggregate good. **Vivid detail:** Elizabeth Hancock burying her husband and six children herself in eight days, August 1666 — the Riley Graves still maintained by the National Trust.

### Philadelphia yellow fever (1793)
~5,000+ dead (10–17% of a fled-down population of 50,000); CFR ~50%. Likely introduced by Saint-Domingue refugees carrying *Aedes aegypti*. **Pivotal because** the federal government literally evacuated the U.S. capital. **Two opposing exemplars:** Benjamin Rush (Declaration signer, blamed rotting coffee, prescribed massive bloodletting and mercury — almost certainly killing many) vs. Stephen Girard (immigrant merchant, personally managed Bush Hill hospital, hired French-trained Jean Devèze who used cooler care with better outcomes). Rush's mistaken belief in Black immunity led the Free African Society (Absalom Jones, Richard Allen) to recruit Black nurses — who died at white rates and were then accused of profiteering. **Foundational American case of politicized medicine:** Federalists said disease was imported (close ports); Republicans said it was local miasma (clean the city).

### 19th-century cholera and John Snow / Broad Street (1854)
1853–54 London pandemic: ~10,738 deaths; Broad Street outbreak: 616 deaths in a 250-yard radius in a few weeks. **Pivotal because** Snow's investigation — combined with Henry Whitehead's door-to-door confirmation — proved waterborne disease before germ theory was established (Pacini identified *V. cholerae* the same year, was ignored; Koch rediscovered it in 1883). **Vivid detail:** Snow had the pump handle removed **September 8, 1854** — but the outbreak was already waning; the durable proof came from his "Grand Experiment" comparing Southwark & Vauxhall vs. Lambeth water-company customers on different Thames sewage intakes. **Hampstead widow** preferred Broad Street water and had it carted to her — died of cholera, as did a visiting niece. Establishment (Edwin Chadwick, General Board of Health) clung to miasma; the Vestry reattached the handle once panic subsided. The case is the founding myth of epidemiology *and* the founding case of **institutional inertia**: correct science loses to dominant paradigms until generational turnover.

### Third plague pandemic (1855– ; San Francisco 1900)
~15 million dead globally, mostly British India and China. Yersin and Kitasato independently isolated *Y. pestis* in Hong Kong June 1894; Paul-Louis Simond proved flea transmission in Karachi 1898. **Pivotal because of San Francisco 1900–04:** federal bacteriologist Joseph Kinyoun confirmed plague in Wong Chut King (March 6, 1900). **Governor Henry Gage**, allied with railroads and Levi Strauss to protect $40M produce trade, denounced Kinyoun as a fraud and got him reassigned. Honolulu Board of Health's controlled burn of Chinatown (Jan 20, 1900) burned out of control, leaving 7,000 Asian residents homeless. Only Rupert Blue's culturally attentive rat-eradication campaign (1903–08) contained the outbreak. **Commerce and racism, not ignorance of bacteriology, drove the failure.**

### 1918–1920 influenza pandemic
50–100 million dead globally (~1/3 of world infected); ~675,000 Americans; U.S. life expectancy dropped 12 years. H1N1, reconstructed by Jeffery Taubenberger (2005) from Alaska permafrost. Origin likely Camp Funston, Kansas (Albert Gitchell, March 4, 1918). **The Philadelphia vs. St. Louis natural experiment is the canonical "flatten the curve" case:** Philadelphia Director Wilmer Krusen permitted the **Liberty Loan Parade on September 28, 1918** (200,000 on Broad Street) under pressure from war-bond officials; within 72 hours every hospital bed was filled; ~12,000+ dead in six weeks, peak weekly excess rate 257/100,000. St. Louis Health Commissioner Max Starkloff closed schools, theaters, and gatherings within 48 hours of first civilian cases October 7; peak 31/100,000; total ~700 deaths. Codified by **Hatchett, Mecher & Lipsitch, *PNAS* 2007**. Wartime censorship in Allied countries suppressed reporting; only neutral Spain reported freely, hence the misnomer.

### Smallpox eradication (certified May 8, 1980)
Smallpox killed ~300 million in the 20th century alone. WHO Intensified Eradication Programme launched 1967 under **D.A. Henderson**. **William Foege's ring vaccination** strategy, developed during the 1966 Biafran outbreak, replaced mass vaccination — proving that surveillance + targeted vaccination of contacts could break transmission with limited resources. **Last natural *V. major* case:** Rahima Banu, age 3, Bangladesh, October 1975. **Last natural case overall: Ali Maow Maalin**, 23, hospital cook in Merca, Somalia, rash appeared October 26, 1977. **Last death: Janet Parker**, medical photographer at Birmingham Medical School, September 11, 1978 — lab leak from Henry Bedson's smallpox research lab one floor below. Bedson slit his throat in his garden shed September 1, 1978, dying September 6, leaving the note "I am sorry to have misplaced the trust." Required Cold War cooperation (USSR proposed the program in 1959; U.S. funded and staffed it). Maalin spent his later life as a polio vaccinator, dying of malaria July 22, 2013, while running a polio campaign in militia territory.

### HIV/AIDS (1981 onward)
First *MMWR* report June 5, 1981 (5 cases of *Pneumocystis* pneumonia in young L.A. gay men). Virus isolated 1983–84 (Montagnier/Barré-Sinoussi at Pasteur; Gallo at NIH). AZT approved 1987; HAART 1996. **Cumulative deaths ~42–44 million (UNAIDS 2024); ~88 million infected; ~39 million currently living with HIV.** **Pivotal because** science raced ahead while social/political response dragged: Reagan did not say "AIDS" publicly until September 17, 1985, after 12,000+ U.S. deaths; first formal speech May 31, 1987. ACT UP, founded by Larry Kramer March 1987 ("Silence = Death"), reshaped FDA fast-tracking and patient representation in clinical trials. **South Africa under Mbeki (1999–2008)** rejected ARVs based on dissident Peter Duesberg's denial; Chigwedere et al. (*JAIDS*, Dec 2008) estimated **>330,000 preventable deaths** and 35,000 infant infections in South Africa alone. The AIDS Quilt — first displayed on the National Mall October 11, 1987 — had grown by 1996 to 40,000+ 3'×6' panels (grave-sized), 800 tons.

### SARS (2002–2003)
8,096 cases, 774 deaths in 29 countries; CFR ~9.6% (up to 50% in over-65s). SARS-CoV-1, bat-origin via civet cats. **Two named figures define the case.** Dr. Liu Jianlun checked into Room 911 of Hong Kong's Metropole Hotel February 21, 2003 and infected guests on the 9th floor whose travel seeded ~half of the world's eventual cases (Toronto cluster, Hanoi, Singapore). **Dr. Carlo Urbani**, WHO infectious-disease officer in Hanoi, examined Johnny Chen on February 28, recognized the threat within days, alerted WHO Manila, contracted SARS on the way to a Bangkok conference, and died March 29, 2003, age 46 — his early warning credited with saving thousands of lives. **Pivotal because** SARS extinguished a pre-pandemic coronavirus via classical public health (quarantine, contact tracing, hospital infection control) *before any vaccine existed* — and triggered the **2005 revision of the International Health Regulations** that China and WHO would invoke 17 years later for COVID-19.

### H1N1 swine flu (2009)
~284,000 estimated deaths globally (range 151,700–575,400; lab-confirmed reported only ~18,500). WHO declared pandemic June 11, 2009. Origin pinpointed to swine in central Mexico; ancestor circulating ≥10 years. **Pivotal because** the WHO declaration triggered automatic European vaccine-purchase contracts; vaccine arrived too late for the peak; Council of Europe inquiry alleged pharma influence (rejected by WHO but damaging to credibility). The first 21st-century pandemic was a successful test of the post-SARS surveillance system *and* a damaging false alarm that left publics primed to disbelieve the next, real warning. **The asymmetry lesson:** the cost of preparing for severe pandemics that turn mild vs. the catastrophic cost of *not* preparing for mild pandemics that turn severe.

### West African Ebola (2014–2016)
**11,325 deaths** of 28,652 cases (CFR ~40%). Index: Emile Ouamouno, 18-month-old, Meliandou, Guinea, December 2013. WHO declared PHEIC August 8, 2014 — five months after recognition, criticized by the Harvard-LSHTM Independent Panel. **The burial-practices conflict is the central scene:** up to 60% of Guinean cases traced to funeral rites (washing, kissing, embracing infectious corpses); a single traditional healer's funeral in Sierra Leone seeded **365 deaths**. The shift came September 2014 when communities accepted Red Cross "Safe and Dignified Burial" teams. The **West Point, Monrovia cordon** (August 20–30, 2014) — armed troops sealing a ~70,000-person slum — produced riots; **15-year-old Shakie Kamara was shot and killed**. In Womey, Guinea (September 16, 2014), **eight sensitization-team members were murdered**. Dr. Stella Adadevoh in Lagos quarantined index-patient Patrick Sawyer at First Consultant Hospital, contracted Ebola, and died August 19, 2014. The **rVSV-ZEBOV vaccine** was ring-trialed in Guinea 2015 with 100% efficacy and licensed in 2019.

### Zika (2015–2016)
PHEIC February 1 – November 18, 2016. ~1.5M Brazilian infections; ~3,500+ congenital Zika syndrome cases by January 2016. **Pivotal because** the PHEIC was declared *not for the infection but for fetal microcephaly* — a teratogen redefining what a public health emergency could be. The 2016 Rio Olympics ran under active PHEIC; despite withdrawals (McIlroy, Day) no Games-linked outbreak materialized. El Salvador advised women to delay pregnancy until 2018, igniting reproductive-rights conflict in restrictive abortion regimes.

### COVID-19 (2019–present)
~7.05M confirmed deaths (WHO); **excess-mortality estimate 14.83M for 2020–21 alone (Msemburi et al., *Nature* 2023); IHME / Economist 18–28M cumulative through 2023**. Global GDP shrank 3.3% in 2020 (worst since Great Depression); IMF projected **$12.5–16 trillion cumulative loss through 2024**; G20 fiscal support reached ~$15 trillion. **Vaccine sequence-to-shot in 326 days** (genome shared Jan 10/11, 2020 → Pfizer EUA Dec 11, 2020). PHEIC declared January 30, 2020; pandemic declared March 11, 2020; PHEIC ended May 5, 2023. **Iconic scenes:** Li Wenliang's death February 7, 2020 (17M watching live); Wuhan lockdown of 11M for 76 days starting January 23, 2020; **Bergamo Army convoy of coffins March 18, 2020** (province had 571% excess mortality that month); NYC refrigerated-truck morgues April 2020; the toilet-paper panic; the April 23, 2020 disinfectant suggestion. The **master case of the science–society gap**: biomedical science at its fastest ever, response crippled by U.S.–China rivalry, vaccine nationalism (~75% of 2021 doses to high-income countries; <2% of low-income populations vaccinated by end-2021), misinformation, and politicization.

## 2. Named figures and institutions

### People (one-line descriptors and a defining detail)

**Thucydides** (c. 460–c. 400 BC). The first epidemiologist; his Plague of Athens narrative — written as a survivor — set the template for every plague account since, including the first recorded note of acquired immunity.

**Lady Mary Wortley Montagu** (1689–1762) & **Onesimus** (c. 1690s–?). Brought variolation to the West: Montagu from Constantinople observation 1717; Onesimus, an enslaved West African, described the African practice to Cotton Mather in Boston, whose 1721 campaign with Zabdiel Boylston produced ~2% variolation CFR vs. ~14% natural CFR. Mather's house was firebombed.

**Edward Jenner** (1749–1823). Invented vaccination: May 14, 1796, inoculated 8-year-old James Phipps with cowpox from dairymaid Sarah Nelmes's hand; coined "vaccine" from Latin *vacca* (cow).

**Benjamin Rush** (1746–1813) & **Stephen Girard** (1750–1831). Opposing exemplars of the 1793 Philadelphia yellow fever response: Rush's bloodletting orthodoxy almost certainly killed; Girard's hands-on hospital management with Jean Devèze saved.

**Ignaz Semmelweis** (1818–1865). Discovered handwashing cut puerperal-fever mortality from ~18% to ~2% in Vienna 1847; mocked, committed to an asylum, died of sepsis after being beaten by guards — the disease he had fought.

**John Snow** (1813–1858). Father of epidemiology; removed the Broad Street pump handle September 8, 1854; also chloroformed Queen Victoria for the birth of Prince Leopold (1853).

**Florence Nightingale** (1820–1910). Cut Crimean War hospital mortality from ~42% to ~2% by reorganizing sanitation; her polar-area "rose diagrams" of British Army deaths were the first weaponized data visualization in public health. Believed in miasma to her death.

**Louis Pasteur** (1822–1895). Built germ theory into a working science: pasteurization, anthrax vaccine (Pouilly-le-Fort 1881), rabies vaccine (saved Joseph Meister July 6, 1885). Pasteur Institute (1888) funded by public subscription.

**Robert Koch** (1843–1910). Father of medical bacteriology: *Bacillus anthracis* 1876, tubercle bacillus March 24, 1882 (World TB Day), cholera vibrio 1883; Koch's postulates; 1905 Nobel.

**Joseph Lister** (1827–1912). Antiseptic surgery (1865 carbolic acid spray) cut compound-fracture mortality from ~45% to ~15%; first surgeon raised to the peerage; Listerine and *Listeria* both named for him.

**Paul Ehrlich** (1854–1915). "Magic bullet": Salvarsan, compound #606, first antimicrobial (1909); shared 1908 Nobel.

**Walter Reed** (1851–1902) & **Carlos Finlay**. Proved *Aedes aegypti* transmits yellow fever, Cuba 1900; one team member (Jesse Lazear) died in human-volunteer trials; enabled the Panama Canal.

**Mary Mallon, "Typhoid Mary"** (1869–1938). First identified asymptomatic typhoid carrier in North America; ≥51 infections, 3 deaths; forcibly confined 26 years total (1907–10, 1915–38) on North Brother Island. Roughly 400 other identified carriers walked free; she was poor, immigrant, female, defiant.

**Hans Zinsser** (1878–1940). Typhus immunologist; *Rats, Lice and History* (1935) taught a generation that lice-borne disease had decided more wars than generals.

**Alexander Fleming** (1881–1955). Discovered penicillin by accident, September 3, 1928; couldn't purify it; Florey, Chain, Heatley at Oxford did, a decade later; shared 1945 Nobel.

**Jonas Salk** (1914–1995). Inactivated polio vaccine: results announced April 12, 1955, church bells rang nationwide. Asked who held the patent: "Could you patent the sun?"

**Albert Sabin** (1906–1993). Oral live-attenuated polio vaccine; tested it on himself and his family; trialed on 10 million Soviet children in 1959 when the U.S. balked. Also refused to patent.

**Maurice Hilleman** (1919–2005). Developed 40+ vaccines (8 of the 14 routine U.S. childhood shots); when his daughter Jeryl Lynn caught mumps in 1963 he swabbed her throat at midnight — the "Jeryl Lynn strain" is still in MMR today.

**D.A. Henderson** (1928–2016). Led WHO smallpox eradication 1966–77; later skeptical of polio eradication; founding director Hopkins Center for Civilian Biodefense; Presidential Medal of Freedom.

**William Foege** (1936–2026). Invented ring vaccination in Eastern Nigeria, December 1966 — proved smallpox could be eradicated with as little as 7% population coverage. Later CDC director (1977–83); over 2m tall.

**Donald R. Hopkins** (b. 1941). Drove Guinea worm cases from ~3.5 million in 1986 to ~10 in 2024 — without drug or vaccine. MacArthur Fellow.

**Larry Brilliant** (b. 1944). Smallpox eradicator in South Asia (advised to take the job by his guru Neem Karoli Baba); 2006 TED Prize for pandemic early warning. "Outbreaks are inevitable, pandemics are optional."

**Anthony Fauci** (b. 1940). Director of NIAID 1984–2022; clashed with then collaborated with ACT UP; the face of U.S. COVID messaging; advised seven presidents.

**Peter Piot** (b. 1949). Co-discoverer of Ebola (1976, age 27, in Antwerp); founding executive director of UNAIDS (1995–2008); director LSHTM 2010–21.

**Paul Farmer** (1959–2022). Co-founder of Partners In Health (1987); drove HIV ARV prices from ~$20,000 to ~$80 per patient-year; died February 21, 2022, in Butaro, Rwanda. Subject of *Mountains Beyond Mountains*.

**Carlo Urbani** (1956–2003). Identified SARS in Hanoi February 28, 2003; died of it March 29, 2003.

**Li Wenliang** (1985/86–2020). Wuhan ophthalmologist whistleblower; warning shared December 30, 2019; forced to sign "spreading rumours" statement January 3, 2020; died February 7, 2020. Posthumously exonerated.

**Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus** (b. 1965). First African and first non-physician WHO Director-General (since July 1, 2017); declared COVID-19 PHEIC January 30, 2020 and pandemic March 11, 2020; popularized the term "infodemic" (Munich Security Conference, February 2020).

**Katalin Karikó** (b. 1955) **& Drew Weissman** (b. 1959). 2023 Nobel for the 2005 modified-nucleoside discovery that made mRNA vaccines possible. Karikó was demoted at Penn in 1995; her foundational 2005 paper was rejected by *Nature* and *Science*. The 61st woman to win a science Nobel.

## 3. Key concepts the generalist reader needs

### Germ theory vs. miasma theory
Miasma ("bad air") reigned from Hippocrates through the mid-1800s. The pivot occurred ~1850–1880: Snow (1854) on cholera, Semmelweis (1840s) on puerperal fever, Pasteur (1860s–70s) on fermentation and disease, Koch (1876–83) on the anthrax, TB, and cholera bacilli. **Florence Nightingale, hero of sanitation, died still believing in miasma** — illustrating that practical public health (cleaner water, better sewers) often worked despite wrong theory.

### R₀, Rₜ, and herd immunity
**R₀** = average secondary infections from one case in a fully susceptible, well-mixed population; not a biological constant but varies with density, behavior, and method. **Rₜ** = the same metric at time *t* with partial immunity and interventions; the operational target is to push Rₜ below 1. **Herd immunity threshold = 1 − 1/R₀**: smallpox (R₀ 5–7) needed ~80–85% coverage; measles (R₀ 12–18) needs **93–95%** — which is why measles is the first to roar back when coverage slips. Concept coined by veterinarians c. 1916 ("herd"); Hedrich documented natural herd immunity to measles in 1930s Baltimore.

### The epidemic curve and "flattening the curve"
The two-curve graphic was popularized in the 2007 CDC Community Mitigation Strategy and refined by **Drew Harris** (February 2020). Its scientific spine is **Hatchett, Mecher & Lipsitch, *PNAS* 2007** — 17 U.S. cities in 1918, early multi-NPI cities had peak death rates ~50% lower. **Richard Hatchett later became CEO of CEPI.**

### NPIs, contact tracing, ring vaccination
Non-pharmaceutical interventions: quarantine (exposed asymptomatic), isolation (known infected), social distancing, masking, school/business closures, travel restrictions, cordon sanitaire, hand hygiene, ventilation, digital exposure notification. Contact tracing works at low case counts and collapses at exponential spread. **Ring vaccination** — Foege's smallpox strategy — was reused for Ebola (rVSV-ZEBOV, 2015) and mpox (2022).

### Variolation vs. vaccination
**Variolation** = deliberate smallpox-material inoculation; ~1–2% CFR vs. ~30% natural. Lady Mary Wortley Montagu (London 1721) and Onesimus/Cotton Mather/Zabdiel Boylston (Boston 1721) introduced it to the Anglosphere. **Vaccination** (Jenner 1796) used cowpox — same immunity, far safer.

### Zoonotic spillover
~60% of known human pathogens and **~75% of *emerging* infectious diseases are zoonotic** (Taylor, Latham & Woolhouse, *Phil Trans R Soc B*, 2001 — canonical citation). Drivers: deforestation, livestock intensification, wildlife trade, urban encroachment, climate change. Examples: HIV (chimps), Ebola (likely bats), SARS-1 (bats via civets), MERS (bats via camels), Nipah, Lassa, Zika, COVID-19.

### Antigenic drift vs. shift
**Drift** = gradual point mutations in HA/NA; why flu vaccines are reformulated annually. **Shift** = abrupt segment reassortment (often in pigs as "mixing vessel") producing a novel subtype — the pandemic trigger (1918 H1N1, 1957 H2N2, 1968 H3N2, 2009 H1N1pdm).

### Endemic vs. epidemic vs. pandemic
Endemic = constant baseline (malaria in sub-Saharan Africa); epidemic = sudden rise above expected, wider scale; pandemic = sustained community transmission across multiple countries/continents. WHO has no single numeric threshold; the call is judgment-based.

### CFR vs. IFR
**CFR** = deaths/diagnosed cases (sensitive to testing intensity). **IFR** = deaths/total infections (true biological lethality, requires serology). COVID-19 reported CFR varied 1–15% early; modeled IFR ~0.5–1.0% overall, age-stratified from <0.01% in children to >10% in 80+. Ebola CFR ~50%. Untreated rabies ≈100%. Seasonal flu IFR ~0.1%.

### Surveillance
**Passive** (provider reporting); **active** (proactive case-finding); **sentinel** (e.g., CDC's ILINet); **syndromic** (ED visits, OTC sales); **genomic** (GISAID, UK COG-UK); **wastewater** (independent of clinical testing; CDC NWSS expanded from a handful of sites in 2020 to >1,500 across all 50 states by 2024 — H5N1 wastewater signal in 2024 confirmed spread before farm reporting in several states).

### One Health, cordon sanitaire, Swiss cheese model, Disease X
**One Health**: WHO/FAO/WOAH/UNEP quadripartite framework (2022) — human, animal, environmental health are interdependent. **Cordon sanitaire**: coined 1821 (French troops at the Pyrenees against yellow fever); used in West African Ebola 2014, Wuhan 2020. **Swiss cheese model** (James Reason, 1990s industrial safety; adapted to COVID by Ian Mackay, October 2020): no single defense is perfect but stacked layers block most pathways. **"Disease X"**: WHO 2018 placeholder for the next unknown pandemic pathogen; COVID-19 was the first realized Disease X.

### Vaccine platforms
Live attenuated (MMR, OPV, yellow fever, varicella) — strong, often one dose, not for immunocompromised. Inactivated (IPV, hep A, many flu, Sinovac/Sinopharm). Subunit/recombinant (hep B, HPV, Novavax COVID, shingles). Toxoid (tetanus, diphtheria). Viral vector (J&J and AstraZeneca COVID; rVSV Ebola). **mRNA** (Pfizer-BioNTech, Moderna COVID, Dec 2020) — design within days of sequence, no live pathogen, cold-chain demanding.

## 5. Recent developments (2020–2026)

### mRNA breakthrough and Operation Warp Speed
Karikó & Weissman's 2005 modified-nucleoside paper (rejected by *Nature* and *Science* at the time) won the **2023 Nobel** October 2, 2023. SARS-CoV-2 genome shared January 10/11, 2020; Pfizer-BioNTech FDA EUA December 11, 2020 — **326 days**, vs. ~1,800 days for the prior record (mumps). **Operation Warp Speed**, announced May 15, 2020, scaled from ~$10B to ~$18B by October 2020. Platform expansion: Moderna mRESVIA (RSV) approved May 2024; mNEXSPIKE (next-gen COVID) approved May 31, 2025; combo flu/COVID targeted 2026; cancer vaccines (mRNA-4157, mRNA-1647) in Phase 3.

### COVID-19 origins: unresolved
**Two hypotheses**, neither dispositively proven. **Lab-leak**: focuses on Wuhan Institute of Virology, gain-of-function research, NIH funding via EcoHealth Alliance (debarred January 2025), and the unusual furin cleavage site. **Zoonotic**: Huanan Seafood Market; raccoon-dog metagenomic samples (March 2023) suggestive; Worobey/Andersen/Garry *Science* papers (July 2022) argued for spillover. **Key assessments**: WHO-China joint study (March 2021) "extremely unlikely" lab leak (broadly criticized as constrained); ODNI October 2021 — IC split; **DOE** low-confidence lab (Feb 2023); **FBI** moderate-confidence lab; **CIA January 25, 2025** shifted to low-confidence research-related origin under exit-Burns/incoming-Ratcliffe review (no new intel); House Select Subcommittee final report (December 2024) favored research-related. **Proximal Origin paper** (Andersen et al., *Nature Medicine* March 17, 2020): subpoenaed Slack/email evidence (2023–24) showed authors privately favored lab origin; Wellcome's Jeremy Farrar used a burner phone for the discussions. Petition >1,700 calls for retraction; *Nature Medicine* has not retracted. **No scientific consensus as of May 2026.**

### WHO Pandemic Agreement
INB launched December 2021; missed 2024 WHA deadline. **Pandemic Agreement adopted by consensus at the 78th WHA on May 20, 2025** (Resolution WHA78.1) — only WHO's second-ever binding treaty (after FCTC tobacco). **Not yet open for signature**: Article 31 requires the **Pathogen Access and Benefit-Sharing (PABS) annex** (Article 12) to be adopted first; IGWG negotiations through Jan 2026 failed to finalize; on May 1, 2026, WHO announced an extension; the 79th WHA (May 18–23, 2026) will be asked to authorize continuation through May 2027. **U.S. withdrew** under Trump EO 14155 (January 20, 2025); Argentina also withdrew; Israel and others abstained. Contested PABS provision: 20% of pandemic-product output to WHO (10% donation + 10% affordable) in exchange for rapid pathogen-sequence sharing. Related: **78th WHA on June 1, 2024 adopted IHR (2005) amendments** creating a new "pandemic emergency" category.

### Pandemic preparedness funding
G20 HLIP (2021): $15B/yr recommended. **Pandemic Fund** at the World Bank (approved June 30, 2022): three funding rounds; ~$11.5B cumulative across 128 countries by February 2026 (with 1:7 co-financing leverage over $1.4B in direct grants); 67 projects implemented. Third round announced Kigali November 19, 2025 ($500M, 20 projects, 32 LMICs). Demand vastly exceeds supply; 2024 replenishment campaign ($2B target) reached ~$1B by October 2024. The "panic-and-neglect" cycle (Jim Yong Kim, 2017) is repeating post-COVID, intensified by 2025 U.S. retrenchment.

### H5N1 bird flu in U.S. dairy cattle and humans (2024–2026)
**First detection in dairy cattle: March 25, 2024**, Texas. Genotype B3.13 (clade 2.3.4.4b). First human dairy-linked case (Texas farmworker, conjunctivitis): April 1, 2024. **Spread to ~973+ herds across 17+ states** (CIDRAP late 2025). California: 291+ herds; quarantine lifted February 27, 2026 (still NMTS Stage 3). **70 confirmed U.S. human cases**; mostly mild dairy-worker conjunctivitis. **Genotype D1.1** first in Alaska wild duck October 6, 2024; caused severe illness in a BC teen (Nov 2024) and the **first U.S. H5N1 death: a Louisiana resident over 65 with backyard-poultry exposure, hospitalized December 2024, died January 6, 2025**. Mexico's first H5N1 death: Durango child, March 2025. D1.1 independently spilled into dairy cattle in Nevada and Arizona (January–February 2025) — the second independent cattle spillover. USDA delayed mandatory milk testing until December 6, 2024; CDC moved to monthly reporting July 7, 2025; serosurvey of 150 bovine vets (CDC Sept 2024) found 3 asymptomatic positives. ~4.8M doses bulk H5 vaccine stockpiled by spring 2025; Moderna mRNA-1018 H5 in trials; **no cattle vaccine in the U.S.** CDC risk assessment: "low"; no sustained human-to-human transmission. Trump-administration CDC layoffs and database pauses have eroded surveillance.

### Erosion of trust
KFF tracking: U.S. CDC trust 85% pre-pandemic → 66% June 2023 → 61% January 2025 → **47% early 2026**. Democrats' CDC trust: 88% (Sep 2023) → 55% (early 2026) — a sign of cross-partisan erosion under the new HHS regime. **2025 measles**: outbreak began January 2025 in Gaines County, Texas Mennonite community; spread to NM, OK, KS, Chihuahua; **762 cases, 3 deaths** by Texas end-of-outbreak August 18, 2025; Chihuahua linked outbreak: 3,854 cases, 13 deaths. U.S. exceeded **1,356 cases by August 5, 2025** — worst since 1992 (elimination declared 2000). RFK Jr. promoted vitamin A over MMR.

### Infodemic and misinformation
Term popularized by Tedros, **Munich Security Conference, February 2020**. **Disinformation Dozen** (CCDH, March 2021): 12 accounts allegedly produced ~65% of anti-vaccine social-media content — figure disputed by Meta (later said named accounts were ~0.05% of vaccine-related views). **Platform retreats**: Twitter/X under Musk (Oct 2022) rolled back COVID misinfo policy and reinstated banned accounts; **Meta ended third-party fact-checking January 7, 2025**, replacing with Community Notes.

### CEPI 100 Days Mission
Goal: vaccine for new pathogen within 100 days of identification (vs. 326 for COVID). Endorsed by G7 (June 2021) and G20. Modeling: 100 Days for COVID could have averted **8.3M excess deaths** and $1.4T productivity loss by end-2021. **CEPI 2.0** (2022–26): $3.5B target; **CEPI 3.0** launched March 2025. 2025 IPPS scorecard: R&D funding declined sharply since 2022; ~25 priority viral families still lack complete DTV pipelines; **U.S. government provided 60% of all DTV funding 2014–2023** — concentration now a risk given 2025 retrenchment.

## 7. Recurring patterns: the science-vs-society gap

The book's organizing thesis can be developed through **twelve recurring patterns**, each anchored in named, dated cases:

**Denial and delay.** Justinian taxing the depopulated countryside; Gage denying SF plague 1900–02; Reagan's 1981–85 silence; Mbeki's 1999–2008 denialism (~330,000 dead); Wuhan reprimanding Li Wenliang January 2020. Structure: economic interests + ideological commitment + leaders' optimism bias.

**Scapegoating of minorities.** Jews 1348 (~350 pogroms, Strasbourg Feb 14, 1349 ~2,000 dead, Mainz ~6,000); Irish in 1832 New York cholera; Chinese in 1900 SF and 2020 COVID (anti-Asian hate crimes +339% in 2021 per CSUSB); Haitians in early AIDS (CDC's "4H" risk groups, FDA donor ban); gay men in AIDS ("GRID" name 1982).

**Liberty vs. collective safety.** *Jacobson v. Massachusetts*, 197 U.S. 11 (1905) upholding compulsory smallpox vaccination 7–2 (Harlan: "manifold restraints… for the common good"); the Anti-Mask League of San Francisco (1919, ~5,000 members); 2020 mask wars; *NFIB v. OSHA* (2022) partial retreat from Jacobson tradition.

**Politicization of public health.** Federalists vs. Republicans on yellow fever 1793 (imported vs. local); COVID red/blue mortality divergence post-vaccine (Wallace et al. *PNAS* 2022; Bollyky et al. *Lancet* 2023).

**Trust as the critical variable.** Bollyky et al. *Lancet* (2022, 177 countries): interpersonal and government trust beat democracy, GDP, healthcare capacity, and PPR indices as predictors of COVID outcomes. Counterfactual: Denmark-level trust globally → 13% fewer infections. U.S. state-level (Lancet 2023): 1-SD interpersonal trust = 12.9% mortality decrease.

**Panic-and-neglect cycle.** Coined by World Bank President Jim Yong Kim, May 25, 2017 ("From Panic and Neglect to Investing in Health Security"). Documented post-SARS 2003, post-H1N1 2009, post-Ebola 2014–16 (PEFF underfunded), post-COVID (treaty stalled 2024–25; CDC cuts 2025).

**Science-policy gap.** Krusen vs. Liberty Loan committee 1918; Surgeon General Rupert Blue subordinated to wartime mobilization; C. Everett Koop's 1986 AIDS report delayed by White House staff; UK's "controlled spread" reversed by Imperial College's March 16, 2020 modeling.

**Misinformation patterns.** Well-poisoning rumors 1348; "the French disease"; Wakefield MMR/autism (1998, retracted 2010 — UK MMR fell from 92% to <80%, cases 56 in 1998 → 1,370 in 2008); the Disinformation Dozen; "plandemic." Common features: simple causal stories, named enemies, emotional resonance over evidence, hidden material motives.

**Economic disruption hits the poor.** Hamburg 1892 cholera ~20× mortality gap by district (Evans, *Death in Hamburg*); 1918 flu in tenements; AIDS shift to Black women and IV drug users by late 1990s; U.S. COVID Black/Hispanic mortality ~2× white; meatpacking, transit, and care workers; the West Point cordon punishing the slum's residents.

**Health workers as heroes and scapegoats.** Wuhan's first wave; "clap for carers"; vs. Womey murders (Sept 16, 2014); Henry Bedson's suicide; 2020 nurses harassed in scrubs.

**Blame the victim.** Medieval lepers as sinners; 19th-century Contagious Diseases Acts (1864/66/69) forcibly examining women suspected of prostitution; gay men and AIDS "lifestyle"; obese patients in early COVID; the elderly in nursing homes. Sontag: "Plague has been used metaphorically as the highest standard of collective calamity… and as a sign of moral pollution."

**Communication failures (Three Mile Island pattern).** Covello/Sandman risk-communication: officials downplay early, lose credibility, can't regain it later. TMI March 28, 1979; Chernobyl 1986 (36-hour Soviet denial; Sweden detected radiation first); SARS 2003 China cover-up; Wuhan late December 2019; CDC mask guidance reversal 2020. **Sandman: Risk = Hazard + Outrage.**

## Key caveats for the TOC designer

1. **The "SS Eldorado" detail in the original brief appears nowhere in standard Mary Mallon biographies** — likely a confusion with another quarantine vessel. Mallon was held on **North Brother Island**.
2. **Plague of Athens pathogen** remains disputed; aDNA favors typhoid (Papagrigorakis 2006) but clinical match is contested.
3. **1918 flu death toll**: "50–100 million" is the conventional range; Spreeuwenberg et al. (2018) revised downward to ~17M but acknowledged LMIC underestimation.
4. **COVID-19 deaths**: confirmed (~7M) and excess (15–28M) figures differ by ~3×; cite both ranges with methodology.
5. **Bollyky trust findings** are correlational; flag epistemic humility.
6. **Lab-leak vs. zoonosis** has no scientific consensus as of May 2026; treat as ongoing case study of the science–society gap itself.
7. **Eyam pre-plague population** is variously given as ~350 to ~800; ~260 deaths consistent across sources.
8. **Chigwedere's 330,000 South Africa figure** is widely cited but a single 2008 study; Nattrass's separate work gives 343,000.
9. **Fort Pitt smallpox blankets**: documentary intent unambiguous; actual causal contribution to Ohio Valley epidemic contested (Fenn's *Pox Americana* argues plausibly for some role).
10. **Bedson death date**: most reliable sources give September 6, 1978 (5 days after the September 1 throat-cutting).

## Suggested chapter clusters for 20–30 chapter design

A TOC designer can draw on five natural narrative arcs that the material supports:

1. **Antiquity to medieval foundations (4–5 chapters)** — Athens, Antonine, Justinian, Black Death + the invention of quarantine, Jewish pogroms.
2. **From miasma to germ theory (4–5 chapters)** — Columbian Exchange/Amherst blankets, Eyam, Philadelphia yellow fever 1793, cholera and Snow, Semmelweis/Pasteur/Koch, San Francisco plague.
3. **The 20th-century apparatus (5–6 chapters)** — 1918 flu and Philadelphia/St. Louis, Typhoid Mary, the rise of WHO/CDC/EIS, smallpox eradication, Cutter and Tuskegee, the 1976 swine flu fiasco, HIV/AIDS and ACT UP/Mbeki.
4. **The 21st-century stress tests (5–6 chapters)** — SARS 2003, H1N1 2009, Ebola 2014–16, Zika, COVID-19 origins/mRNA/lockdowns/vaccine nationalism, the infodemic.
5. **The present and the next pandemic (4–5 chapters)** — H5N1 2024–26, WHO Pandemic Agreement, the panic-and-neglect cycle, the post-2025 retrenchment, CEPI's 100 Days Mission, recurring patterns and what to do differently.

The unifying thread — the **gap between what epidemiologists know and what societies do** — is best made visible by alternating "knowledge" chapters (Snow, Koch, mRNA, R₀) with "response" chapters (Strasbourg pogroms, Tuskegee, Mbeki, the 2025 vaccine schedule rollback), and by returning repeatedly to the canonical anchor experiments: **Philadelphia vs. St. Louis** and **Eyam**.
