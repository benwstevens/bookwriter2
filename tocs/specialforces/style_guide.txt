VOICE AND TONE:
Write in first person. The author is someone who was "in the room" at the command centers when these missions were unfolding. The tone is conversational but authoritative — like a mentor
explaining the business over coffee. Use "I" freely. Share personal war stories.
Avoid academic jargon but don't talk down to the reader.

TARGET AUDIENCE:
11-year old boys who are ahead of the curve and very knowledgeable but still 11. Work hard to keep the stories engaging and narrative non-fiction-esque, with drama and tension, not wikipedia re-hased. **Because of this audience, you MUST AVOID any explicit references to torture or war crimes.** To the extent they occurred and are part of the story, reference unprofessional conduct or unnecessary roughness. You don't have to act like nothing bad happened but do not give gory or disturbing details.

READING LEVEL:
Aim for a 9th-grade reading level. Short sentences when making key points.
Longer sentences are fine for narrative passages, but break up any paragraph
that runs past 5 sentences.

ANECDOTES:
Every chapter should real-world stories or examples, always drawn from real situations. If a metaphor is ever a composite, identify it as such.

METAPHORS:
Use metaphors drawn from everyday life to explain industry concepts. Avoid
clichés ("at the end of the day," "it's a marathon not a sprint").

DEFAULT WORD COUNT:
1,500 words per chapter unless otherwise specified.

HUMOR:
Light humor is encouraged — wry observations about the crazy tasks at hand. Never at the expense of any group of people. Self-deprecating humor about the author's own mistakes is great.

STRUCTURE:
Follow the default chapter structure unless a chapter's content calls for
something different. The body sections should use descriptive subheadings
(not generic ones like "Section 1").
