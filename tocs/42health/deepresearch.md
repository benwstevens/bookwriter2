# The 42-year-old's longevity playbook: a research dossier

A 42-year-old man stands on a cliff edge that is invisible from where he is standing. His VO2max is declining about 10% per decade, his Type II muscle fibers are quietly atrophying, his slow-wave sleep is shrinking, his arteries already hold the early architecture of atherosclerosis, and his subjective well-being is statistically near its lifetime low. None of this hurts yet. **All of it is more reversible at 42 than at 62**, and most of the reversal comes from a small, boring set of interventions whose evidence is overwhelming when you actually read it.

This dossier organizes that evidence by domain — what's happening in the body, which exercise interventions matter most, the supporting pillars (sleep, nutrition, mental health, social, alcohol, screening), and the named figures and studies that anchor each claim. Throughout, the goal is to separate what's well-supported from what's hype, and to give specific numbers, dates, and effect sizes so each chapter can be drafted directly from the research.

## Part I — What's actually happening to a 42-year-old body

### 1. Sarcopenia is already underway, and it's a Type II fiber problem

**Core claim.** Skeletal muscle mass declines roughly **3–8% per decade after age 30**, accelerating to 10–15%/decade after 60. But mass loss is the trailing indicator: **strength declines 2–5× faster** ("dynapenia"), driven by selective atrophy of Type II (fast-twitch) fibers and motor-unit dropout that begins in the 30s–40s.

The anchoring work is **Lexell's autopsy series** (J Neurol Sci 1988; J Gerontol A 1995) showing limb muscles 25–35% smaller in elderly men with ~25% fewer fibers, dominated by Type II loss. **Nilwik 2013 (Exp Gerontol)** found Type II fibers were 10–40% smaller in older adults while Type I size was preserved. **Stuart Phillips (McMaster)** has anchored the protein side: the **Morton 2018 BJSM meta-analysis** of 49 RCTs (n=1,863) identified a plateau at **~1.62 g protein/kg/day** for fat-free mass gains, with effects amplified in the resistance-trained.

The **PURE study** (Leong 2015, Lancet, n=142,861 across 17 countries) established grip strength as a mortality predictor of unusual power: **each 5 kg lower grip = 16% higher all-cause mortality**, and grip strength predicted death better than systolic blood pressure. This was replicated in UK Biobank (Celis-Morales 2018).

**The insider truth.** Sarcopenia is treated as a geriatric diagnosis, but the mechanisms — motor-unit loss, Type II atrophy, anabolic resistance — are measurable in the 40s. The Morton 1.62 g/kg figure is a *floor* for fat-free mass benefit, not an upper limit; **Bandegan 2017** using indicator amino acid oxidation found requirements of ~1.7 g/kg in trained men with upper bounds near 2.2 g/kg. Importantly, **mass and strength dissociate**: a 42-year-old can have normal DXA lean mass but already-degraded force production.

The "muscle as endocrine organ" frame is real and underappreciated. Bente Pedersen's work (Physiol Rev 2008) showed contracting muscle releases **IL-6 up to 100-fold**; Bruce Spiegelman's lab identified **irisin** (Nature 2012) browning white fat via the PGC1-α–FNDC5 axis; muscle also releases BDNF, IL-15, IL-10, and meteorin-like. Inactive muscle isn't just smaller — it is **endocrinologically silent**, which Pedersen frames as the "diseasome of physical inactivity."

**Recent developments.** The 2024 Lancet sarcopenia seminar (Sayer/Cruz-Jentoft) classified sarcopenia formally as a muscle disease (ICD-10 M62.84). The Global Leadership Initiative in Sarcopenia (Kirk 2024) is unifying definitions. **Zhao 2026 (Front Nutr)** meta-analysis of 39 cohorts found sarcopenia OR 1.79 for all-cause mortality. Myostatin pathway drugs (bimagrumab, apitegromab) increase lean mass without proportional strength gain — reinforcing the mass≠function gap.

**Practical takeaway.** A 42-year-old is in the middle of an asymptomatic erosion window: ~5–15% of muscle mass and 10–20% of strength is already gone vs. his 25-year-old peak. Resistance training 2–3×/week with progressive overload is the only intervention that preserves Type II fibers. Track grip strength annually; clinical low cutoff for men is <27 kg per EWGSOP2.

### 2. VO2max: the most powerful single mortality biomarker

**Core claim.** VO2max declines roughly **10% per decade** after 30 in the sedentary, accelerating in the 60s. **Low cardiorespiratory fitness carries a higher mortality hazard than smoking, diabetes, hypertension, or established CAD.**

The signature study is **Mandsager 2018 (JAMA Network Open)**: Cleveland Clinic, 122,007 patients, treadmill testing 1991–2014, 8.4-year median follow-up. Low fitness (<25th percentile) vs. Elite (≥97.7th): **HR 5.04 (4.10–6.20)**. For comparison: smoking HR 1.41, diabetes HR 1.40, CAD HR 1.29, hypertension HR 1.36, ESRD HR 1.71. **Crucially: no upper limit of benefit** — Elite vs. High HR 0.77 (p=0.02), refuting the U-shaped concerns about excessive endurance training.

**Steven Blair's Aerobics Center Longitudinal Study** (JAMA 1989, Cooper Institute) established the foundational "fit but fat" finding. **Kodama 2009 JAMA** meta-analysis of 33 cohorts: each 1-MET increment = 13% lower all-cause mortality. **Kokkinos 2022 (J Am Coll Cardiol)** in >750,000 US veterans replicated the monotonic relationship across all subgroups. **Lang 2024 BJSM** umbrella review: high vs. low CRF HR 0.47 across 199 cohorts and 20.9 million observations.

**The insider truth.** Peter Attia's framing in *Outlive* (2023) that "VO2max is the strongest single predictor of all-cause mortality" is well-supported but slightly oversimplified — VO2max is an *integrative* biomarker capturing cardiac, vascular, mitochondrial, hematologic, and skeletal-muscle health simultaneously. That's *why* the effect is large, not because it's a single mechanism. About **50–70% of baseline VO2max is genetic** (HERITAGE Family Study, Bouchard), but the trainable response — and roughly half of the age-related decline — is under personal control. Functional independence requires roughly **18 mL/kg/min**; the goal at 42 is to never lose enough buffer to fall below this by 80.

**Practical numbers.** Sedentary 42-year-old male baseline ≈ 35–40 mL/kg/min; fit recreational ≈ 45–55; "elite for age" >55. Attia's prescription: target the 75th percentile of someone a decade younger — for a 42-year-old, **≥48–52 mL/kg/min**. Free estimation tools: NTNU Fitness Calculator (Nes 2011, MSSE; SEE ~5.7 mL/kg/min) or wearable VO2max estimates (Apple Watch, Garmin) within ±3–5 mL/kg/min of CPET.

### 3. Cardiovascular disease is preventable, but the window is closing

**Core claim.** ASCVD remains the **leading cause of death globally (~17–20 million/year)** and in the US (~700,000/year). It is a lifelong process — the **PDAY study** (n≈2,876 trauma autopsies ages 15–34) showed fatty streaks present in essentially all aortas by age 15–19 and raised coronary lesions in 20% of men by 30–34. **Bogalusa Heart Study** confirmed childhood risk-factor burden directly predicts adult lesion extent.

**ApoB beats LDL-C.** **Allan Sniderman (McGill)** has spent decades arguing — with Mendelian-randomization confirmation from Ference, Richardson, and Zuber/Burgess — that apoB is the *causal* lipoprotein particle (one apoB per VLDL, IDL, LDL, Lp(a)). Conditioned on apoB, LDL-C and triglycerides lose independent predictive power. The **2022 ACC Expert Consensus** (Lloyd-Jones, JACC) and the **2024 NLA consensus** formally endorse apoB. Up to ~20% of patients are *discordant* (normal LDL-C but elevated apoB), particularly in metabolic syndrome — and these are systematically under-treated.

**Lp(a) is a once-in-a-lifetime test.** ~80–90% genetically determined, stable across life, unaffected by diet/exercise/statins (statins may slightly raise it). About **20% of the global population** (~1.4 billion people) has Lp(a) >50 mg/dL or >125 nmol/L, conferring ~2× ASCVD risk. The 2022 European Atherosclerosis Society consensus (Kronenberg, Eur Heart J) and 2024 NLA statement both recommend universal once-in-a-lifetime measurement; <5% of US adults have ever had it measured.

**Coronary artery calcium (CAC).** **MESA** (Detrano 2008, NEJM, n=6,814) established CAC's incremental prognostic value. Subsequent **CAC Consortium** work (Blaha, Circulation 2017) and Dzaye 2021 (JACC Cardiovasc Imaging) defined the **"warranty period" of CAC=0 at ~5–7 years** for average-risk patients. Caveat: ~25–33% of incident CVD events still occur with baseline CAC=0 (Mortensen 2019), because soft non-calcified plaque doesn't show up.

**Recent developments.** **CLEAR Outcomes** (Nissen 2023, NEJM, n=13,970) — bempedoic acid reduced MACE-4 by 13% in statin-intolerant patients. **Inclisiran** (twice-yearly siRNA) is in routine use. **Lp(a)-lowering RNAi/ASOs** are in Phase 3: pelacarsen (HORIZON, ~8,000 patients, completes 2026), olpasiran (OCEAN(a), 2027), lepodisiran, and the first oral small-molecule (muvalaplin, JAMA 2023, ~65% reduction). **AI-augmented CCTA** (Cleerly, HeartFlow) increasingly identifies high-risk non-calcified plaque CAC misses; SCOT-HEART 10-year follow-up (Williams 2024) showed durable benefit.

**Practical for a 42-year-old.** Test once: fasting apoB, Lp(a), standard lipid panel, hsCRP, HbA1c, fasting insulin. Consider CAC scan ($100–200, ~1 mSv) if family history of premature CHD, elevated apoB or Lp(a), or borderline 10-year risk. ApoB targets per the Sniderman/Attia school: primary-prevention low-risk <90 mg/dL; high-risk <60–80; high Lp(a) <60. The 42-year-old's defining advantage over a 62-year-old is **time × small-effect compounding** — Mendelian randomization shows lifelong modest apoB lowering produces dramatically larger CVD reduction than the same lowering started at 60.

### 4. Metabolic health: the quiet drift

**Core claim.** By the 40s, insulin resistance is a clinically common, often-undiagnosed cardiometabolic driver. Roughly **38% of US adults have prediabetes** and ~35% meet metabolic syndrome criteria; visceral fat and ectopic liver fat (not BMI) are the proximate drivers.

**Gerald Reaven's 1988 Banting Lecture** (Diabetes 37:1595) introduced "Syndrome X," arguing insulin resistance/hyperinsulinemia clusters with hypertriglyceridemia, low HDL, dysglycemia, and hypertension — independent of LDL. He noted insulin resistance in ~25% of nonobese, normoglycemic individuals. **Hirode & Wong 2020 (JAMA)** found MetS prevalence 34.7% in US adults; trends rising. The CDC 2024 NCHS Data Brief #516 reports prediabetes in 38.0% of US adults (41% men, 32% women); 27.8% of adults 18–44 have prediabetes.

**HOMA-IR is the cheap, underused test.** Fasting insulin × fasting glucose ÷ 405; <1.5 ideal, >2.5–3 typically called impaired. Most primary care orders only fasting glucose and HbA1c — both of which remain normal until β-cells decompensate, often a decade after IR begins. A "normal" fasting glucose of 95 with insulin of 18 yields HOMA-IR ~4.2 (clearly insulin-resistant).

**Visceral adipose tissue is metabolically distinct** — secreting IL-6, TNF-α, and free fatty acids directly into portal circulation. Waist cutoffs (NCEP: >102 cm men; IDF: >94 cm European men) outperform BMI for risk. A 42-year-old man with BMI 26 but waist 41" is metabolically worse off than BMI 28 with waist 36".

**The CGM hype.** Klonoff 2023 (J Diabetes Sci Technol) and the Hall lab's Stanford glucotype work (PLOS Biol 2018) explicitly note evidence in healthy non-diabetics is preliminary. Levels Health and Nutrisense extrapolate beyond evidence; "spikes" in healthy adults often have no demonstrated clinical significance. Educational value is real for 2–4 weeks; treating CGM data as pathologic in someone with normal HbA1c is unsupported.

**MASLD (renamed from NAFLD in 2023)** affects ~30% globally (Younossi/Riazi meta-analyses); 39.7% of men. **Resmetirom (Rezdiffra), FDA approved March 14, 2024** — first-ever drug for noncirrhotic MASH with F2–F3 fibrosis (MAESTRO-NASH, Harrison 2024 NEJM, MASH resolution 26–30% vs. 10% placebo).

**Practical.** Order fasting insulin alongside glucose; compute HOMA-IR. Add ALT (>30 in men suspicious for MASLD), GGT, triglycerides, HDL, apoB, Lp(a). Measure waist; keep <40" (ideally <37"). Use FIB-4 (free calculator from age, AST, ALT, platelets) for liver fibrosis screen; >1.3 → FibroScan. Resistance training plus ≥150 min/week aerobic plus 7%+ weight loss reverses MASLD in many cases.

### 5. Sleep architecture, glymphatics, and the OSA blind spot

**Core claim.** Slow-wave sleep declines markedly across the 30s–60s; sleep-dependent glymphatic clearance is mechanistically linked to Alzheimer's pathology; obstructive sleep apnea is highly prevalent and largely undiagnosed in middle-aged men. **Sleep regularity may matter more than duration.**

**Ohayon 2004 (Sleep)** meta-analyzed 65 PSG studies (n=3,577 ages 5–102): SWS percentage falls roughly linearly across adulthood with steepest declines in midlife (~2 percentage points per decade). **Maiken Nedergaard's Science 2013 paper** (Xie et al.) discovered the **glymphatic system**: CSF–ISF exchange increased ~60% during sleep, β-amyloid clearance doubled. **Lewis lab Boston University** (Fultz 2019, Science) showed humans have large CSF waves during NREM coupled to slow-wave EEG.

**Sabia 2021 (Nature Communications)** — Whitehall II, n=7,959 British civil servants, 25-year follow-up — persistent short sleep (≤6 h) at age 50/60/70 was associated with **30% higher dementia risk** (HR ~1.30), independent of behavioral and cardiometabolic factors.

**Sleep regularity now appears to outweigh duration.** **Windred 2024 (Sleep)** — UK Biobank, n=60,977 with 7-day accelerometry, 1,859 deaths over 7.8 years. Highest vs. lowest Sleep Regularity Index quintile: **HR 0.52 minimal-adjusted, HR 0.70 fully adjusted** for all-cause mortality. **When duration and regularity were modeled together, regularity remained predictive while duration weakened.** Phyllis Zee (Northwestern) and Susan Redline (Brigham) have made parallel observations using MESA data. **Roenneberg 2012 (Curr Biol)** established that ~70% of adults have ≥1 hour of "social jetlag," each hour of which raises overweight odds ~33% in those already overweight.

**OSA is widespread and undiagnosed.** **Peppard 2013 (Am J Epidemiol)** — Wisconsin Sleep Cohort + NHANES — moderate-to-severe SDB (AHI≥15) in ~17% of men 50–70 and ~10% of men 30–49; ~80% remain undiagnosed. **STOP-BANG ≥3** detects AHI ≥15 with 93% sensitivity. A 42-year-old male is automatically +1 (gender) and +1 if BMI >35 or neck >40 cm.

**CPAP's mortality story is weaker than commonly claimed.** **SAVE trial 2016 (McEvoy, NEJM, n=2,717)** in moderate-severe OSA + CV disease: HR 1.10, p=0.34 — **no CV benefit**, with mean adherence only 3.3 h/night. Replicated in ISAACC 2020 and RICCADSA. CPAP clearly improves daytime sleepiness, mood, QoL; mortality benefit appears only in adherent, sleepy patients in observational analyses.

**SURMOUNT-OSA 2024 (Malhotra, NEJM, n=469)** — tirzepatide 10–15 mg weekly reduced AHI by ~25 events/h vs. ~5 with placebo, with 20% body weight loss. **FDA approved tirzepatide (Zepbound) for moderate-severe OSA with obesity in December 2024** — a paradigm shift.

**The Walker controversy.** *Why We Sleep* (2017) is correct on macros but contains specific errors documented by Alexey Guzey's 2019 critique (later discussed by Andrew Gelman) — edited graphs, misattributed statistics, overstated cancer/mortality claims. UC Berkeley conducted a confidential inquiry. **Practical takeaway: the importance of sleep is well-supported, but eye-catching statistics from popular books should not be quoted as gospel.**

**Wearable sleep stages are largely fiction.** Wake/sleep classification is reasonable (κ ~0.30–0.51); 4-stage classification is poor (κ ~0.20–0.28 for Apple Watch, Garmin). Devices systematically over-report time-in-bed as sleep. Use them for trends in regularity, not stage analysis.

**Practical.** Aim for 7+ hours, but the higher-yield target is consistent bed/wake within ±30–60 min including weekends. Take STOP-BANG; ≥3 → request home sleep apnea testing. Avoid alcohol within 3 h of bed. Cool room (60–67°F), dark.

### 6. Brain and cognition: midlife as a leverage point

**Core claim.** Some cognitive domains (processing speed, fluid reasoning, episodic memory) begin measurable decline cross-sectionally in the 20s–30s; crystallized intelligence rises into the 60s+. The leverage is that **midlife vascular and lifestyle factors explain ~45% of dementia risk**.

**Salthouse 2009 (Neurobiol Aging)** showed monotonic decline in fluid abilities from the 20s on cross-sectional data; longitudinal data put the inflection later. Most 42-year-olds remain in the same fluid-cognition range as 32-year-olds; pathological decline (MCI) is rare before 60. **Kirk Erickson's PNAS 2011 trial** in older adults showed 1 year of moderate aerobic exercise increased anterior hippocampal volume by ~2%, with serum BDNF rising correspondingly — though the effect was in 67-year-olds, not 42-year-olds.

**The FINGER trial** (Ngandu 2015, Lancet) — Finland, 2-year RCT in 1,260 at-risk adults aged 60–77 — multidomain intervention (Mediterranean/Nordic diet, exercise, cognitive training, vascular monitoring) improved global cognitive z-score by 0.20 SD vs. control; executive function 25% better, processing speed 83% better. Effect size in absolute terms is modest (Cohen's d ≈ 0.13) but spawned the **World-Wide FINGERS network** in 40+ countries.

**The Lancet Commission on Dementia Prevention (Livingston 2020)** identified 12 modifiable factors accounting for **40% of dementia**. The **2024 update** added untreated vision loss and high LDL — **14 factors and ~45% attributable**. Midlife factors most relevant to a 42-year-old: hypertension, high LDL (newly added), hearing loss, obesity, alcohol, TBI, physical inactivity, smoking.

**US POINTER (JAMA, July 28, 2025)** — first US replication, 2-year RCT in >2,100 adults aged 60–79. Both structured and self-guided multidomain interventions improved cognition; structured arm produced greater global cognition improvement, equivalent to ~1–2 years of normal age-related decline protection. Effects consistent across APOE-ε4 genotype.

**Insider truth.** "Cognitive decline starts at 25" is technically true cross-sectionally for narrow measures but misleadingly conflates noise, cohort effects, and tiny effect sizes. **"40% (or 45%) of dementia is preventable"** is a population-attributable fraction, not an individual probability. White matter hyperintensities increase with midlife hypertension; SPRINT-MIND (JAMA 2019) showed intensive BP control reduces MCI. **Plasma p-tau217 and Aβ42/40** are now FDA-cleared/CLIA-available (2024–2025), bringing AD risk stratification into primary care.

**Practical midlife "modifiable big four":** treat BP to <130/80, treat hearing loss (aids if HL >25 dB), control LDL/apoB, avoid TBI (helmets non-negotiable). Aerobic ≥150 min/week + resistance 2×/week + Mediterranean diet + sleep apnea evaluation.

### 7. Hormonal shifts and the "Low T" mirage

**Core claim.** Total testosterone declines ~1–2% per year after 30, with an additional secular cohort-level decline in American men. Most middle-aged men with low-normal T do **not** have clinical hypogonadism.

**Travison 2007 (JCEM)** documented an age-independent secular decline (~1%/year overlay) in the Massachusetts Male Aging Study — though NHANES data adjusted for adiposity has not confirmed. **Harman 2001 (BLSA)** established the ~1–2%/year longitudinal decline; free T declines faster (~2–3%/year) due to age-related SHBG rise.

**TRAVERSE (Lincoff 2023, NEJM, n=5,246)** — testosterone gel vs. placebo in men 45–80 with documented hypogonadism + symptoms + high CV risk, 21.7-month follow-up. **MACE non-inferior** (7.0% vs. 7.3%). But TRT increased atrial fibrillation (3.5% vs. 2.4%), pulmonary embolism (0.9% vs. 0.5%), AKI (2.3% vs. 1.5%). Snyder 2024 NEJM follow-up: TRT *increased* fracture risk — surprising and contrary to expectations.

**The "Low T" marketing complex is largely disconnected from evidence.** Baillargeon 2018 (JAMA) documented testosterone prescribing exploded after 2002 with DTC advertising. The **FDA's 2015 safety communication** explicitly warned that **TRT is not approved for age-related low T** and required label changes regarding heart attack/stroke risk. Many TRT clinics treat men with T 350–500 ng/dL (well within reference range) based on questionnaires alone.

**The Endocrine Society/Bhasin 2018 guideline** defines hypogonadism as T <264 ng/dL on **two morning fasting LC-MS/MS measurements PLUS specific symptoms** (decreased libido, ED, decreased spontaneous erections, loss of body hair, low energy, decreased exercise tolerance). Most men with "low" T can normalize with **weight loss, sleep optimization, OSA treatment, and resistance training** — Camacho (EMAS, 2013) showed 10% weight loss raises T meaningfully. GLP-1 agonists improve T indirectly via weight loss.

**Practical.** If symptomatic, get morning (8–10 a.m.) fasting total T, SHBG, LH, FSH, prolactin; repeat once if low/borderline; calculate free T. Optimize *before* TRT: weight, sleep, strength training, alcohol, depression, opioids. Avoid online "Low T" clinics. Realistic numbers for 42-year-olds: total T 400–700 ng/dL; "low" usually <300 with symptoms.

## Part II — Exercise, ranked by priority

The dose-response evidence converges on a remarkably simple hierarchy: **strength training and cardiorespiratory fitness deliver the largest mortality dividends, and most of the population-level benefit is captured by surprisingly low doses.**

### 8. Strength training: highest priority, smallest effective dose

**Core claim.** Resistance/muscle-strengthening training delivers an independent, additive reduction in all-cause and CV mortality. **Most of the benefit is achieved at 30–60 minutes per week.**

The signature meta-analysis is **Momma 2022 (BJSM)**: 16 prospective cohorts. Muscle-strengthening activity (MSA) was associated with **15% lower all-cause mortality, 17% lower CVD mortality, 17% lower diabetes mortality, 12% lower cancer mortality**. The dose-response is J-shaped — **maximum benefit at ~30–60 min/week, no further benefit above 60 min/week**. Combined MSA + aerobic vs. neither: **40% lower all-cause, 46% lower CVD, 28% lower cancer mortality**.

**Saeidifard 2019 (Eur J Prev Cardiol, 11 studies, n=370,256)** found any RT vs. none HR 0.79 (21% lower); RT + aerobic HR 0.60 (40% lower); **>2 sessions/week did not add benefit**. **Shailendra 2022 (Am J Prev Med)** confirmed 15% all-cause reduction with maximum benefit at ~60 min/week.

**Volume and frequency.** **Schoenfeld 2017 (J Sports Sci)** dose-response meta-analysis found ~0.37% size increase per additional weekly set, with ≥10 sets/muscle/week producing significantly greater hypertrophy than <5. **Schoenfeld 2016 (Sports Med)** showed 2×/week per muscle was superior to 1×/week when volume was equated. **Pelland 2026 (Sports Med)** confirmed non-linear dose-response with most gains in the 4–12 sets/muscle/week range. **Iversen 2021 (Sports Med)** and **Fyfe 2022** establish a defensible minimum effective dose: a single set of 6–12 reps at 70–85% 1RM, 2–3×/week, near failure, produces meaningful gains in beginners.

**Stuart Phillips on protein.** Morton 2018 BJSM ceiling at 1.62 g/kg/day for FFM; effect attenuated with age (−0.01 kg per year). **Bandegan 2017 (J Nutr)** using IAAO in trained men found requirements of ~1.7 g/kg with upper bound ~2.2 g/kg.

**Insider truth.** The mortality benefit plateau at ~60 min/week is remarkably early. Two 30–45-min full-body sessions per week capture essentially the entire population-level longevity dividend. **Bro-culture volume (15+ hard sets per muscle, 5×/week) is for hypertrophy and aesthetics, not lifespan.** **Volume is the primary lever; frequency is just a way to fit it in.** Grip strength is a *biomarker* of whole-body neuromuscular health, not a target itself — training grip in isolation hasn't been shown to lower mortality.

**Practical for a 42-year-old.** 2–3 full-body sessions/week. Compound lifts: squat or goblet squat, deadlift or hip hinge, bench press, row, overhead press, pull-up. Start at 3–6 hard sets/major muscle/week; build to ~10 sets/muscle/week within 6–12 months. 70–85% 1RM, 6–12 reps, RIR 1–3. Protein ~1.6 g/kg/day, 0.4 g/kg/meal. **Track grip strength annually**; population mean for men 42 is ~46–51 kg dominant, <40 kg is a meaningful flag.

### 9. Zone 2 cardio: mechanistically sound, rhetorically overcooked

**Core claim.** Sustained low-to-moderate aerobic exercise at the highest intensity at which lactate stays below ~2 mmol/L preferentially trains Type I mitochondrial biogenesis, fat oxidation, and lactate clearance — improvements central to insulin sensitivity and aerobic base.

**Iñigo San Millán** (UC Boulder/CU School of Medicine; performance director for UAE Team Emirates / Tadej Pogačar's coach) and **George Brooks** (Berkeley) anchored the modern Zone 2 framing. **San Millán & Brooks 2018 (Sports Med)** demonstrated tight inverse correlation between blood lactate and fat oxidation; pro athletes oxidize fat at far higher absolute power outputs and clear lactate faster. **San Millán 2023 (Antioxidants)** linked mitochondrial dysfunction to T2D, CVD, neurodegeneration, and cancer.

**The 80/20 polarized model.** **Stephen Seiler (Univ. of Agder)** — Seiler & Kjerland 2006 (Scand J Med Sci Sports) quantified that elite endurance athletes accumulate ~80% of training volume below VT1 (Zone 2) with ~20% high intensity. **Stöggl & Sperlich 2014 (Front Physiol)** RCT confirmed polarized produced greater VO2peak gains than threshold-only or HIIT-only over 9 weeks.

**Insider truth.** Zone 2 is more popular than the strict evidence base demands in non-athletes. The strong evidence is for: (i) the *general* MICT → CRF → mortality pathway (Mandsager, Kokkinos); (ii) the polarized 80/20 model in trained athletes (Seiler, Stöggl). The specific claim that "Zone 2" is uniquely superior to other low-intensity work for *health* in middle-aged non-athletes is mechanistically plausible but not directly tested in long-form RCTs. Honest framing: **Zone 2 is a useful operationalization of MICT** that ensures intensity is high enough to drive mitochondrial adaptation without compromising recovery for concurrent strength and HIIT work. **Lactate is fuel, not waste** — Brooks' lactate shuttle is misunderstood by 90% of fitness media.

**Practical Zone 2 definition.** RPE 4–5/10; nasal-only breathing or full-sentence conversation; 60–70% HRmax (untrained), 70–75% HRmax (trained); first ventilatory threshold; lactate ~1.7–2.0 mmol/L if measured. **Volume:** 3–4 hours/week in 45–60 min sessions, 3–4×/week (Attia practical recommendation, consistent with 80/20 logic). Modalities: cycling, rowing, incline treadmill (3.0–3.4 mph at 12–15% grade is Attia's standard), rucking, swimming.

### 10. VO2max and the Norwegian 4×4

**Core claim.** Because central cardiac adaptations (stroke volume, end-diastolic volume) require near-maximal cardiac output to drive, **high-intensity intervals — particularly the Norwegian 4×4 — are the most time-efficient stimulus to raise VO2max.** One to two sessions per week is sufficient.

**Helgerud 2007 (MSSE)** at NTNU: 4 protocols over 8 weeks, n=40 moderately trained men. The **4×4 (4 min at 90–95% HRmax × 4, 3-min recovery at 70%)** increased VO2max by 7.2% (≈+5.5 mL/kg/min) and stroke volume by ~10%, beating continuous protocols. **Wisløff 2007 (Circulation)** in heart-failure patients: 4×4 produced VO2peak +46% vs. +14% for MICT.

**Generation 100 (Stensvold 2020, BMJ)** — 1,567 Trondheim residents aged 70–77, 5-year RCT, 2:1:1 (control / MICT / HIIT). **5-year mortality: control 4.7%, MICT 5.9%, HIIT 3.0%**; HIIT vs. control HR 0.63 (95% CI 0.33–1.20), absolute risk reduction 1.7 pp — **trend favoring HIIT but not statistically significant** because the trial was underpowered (observed mortality lower than projected). **VO2peak gains were greatest in HIIT throughout.** Letnes 2022 (Eur Heart J) follow-up confirmed largest 5-year VO2peak change in HIIT (~+0.76 mL/kg/min).

**The Generation 100 mortality result is widely misrepresented.** Headlines saying "HIIT didn't reduce mortality" miss that the HR was 0.63 — a 37% relative risk reduction trend that simply didn't reach significance. The honest read: HIIT clearly raised VO2peak more than MICT, and VO2peak is itself one of the most robustly mortality-linked variables in medicine.

**Sprint Interval Training (SIT, 30 s × 4–6) is NOT equivalent for VO2max.** Aerobic energy systems have ~2-min on-kinetics; **Wen 2019 (BJSM)** meta-analysis confirmed work bouts >2 min and total interval volume >15 min produce greater VO2max gains. The 4×4 (16 min total) sits in the optimum.

**FIT-AGEING (Amaro-Gahete, Univ. Granada, 2019/2022)** in sedentary 45–65-year-olds confirmed HIIT improves body composition, VO2max (~14% gain), and HRV equivalently or better than concurrent training in less time.

**Practical 4×4 protocol.** 10-min warm-up at 60–70% HRmax → 4 × (4 min at 90–95% HRmax + 3 min active recovery at 60–70%) → 5-min cool-down. Total ~38–40 min, 16 min at high intensity. 1–2×/week, separated by ≥48 h. Cycling, rower, incline treadmill, ski-erg, hill repeats. **Target: 75th percentile of one's age decade — for 42, ≥46 mL/kg/min minimum, ideally 48–52.**

### 11. Mobility, stability, and injury-proofing

**Core claim.** The 40s are an inflection point where connective-tissue stiffening, accumulated joint mileage, and selective atrophy of stabilizers begin to compound. **Peter Attia's "Centenarian Decathlon" framework** in *Outlive* (2023) operationalizes this by backcasting today's training from desired late-life capacities — picking up a 30-lb grandchild, hiking 1.5 miles on hilly terrain, getting off the floor with one point of support, balancing 30 s on one leg.

**Araujo 2022 (BJSM)** — CLINIMEX cohort, n=1,702, ages 51–75, 7-year follow-up. **Inability to complete a 10-second one-legged stance: HR ~1.84 for all-cause mortality**; 20.4% failed; 7-year mortality 17.5% (failing) vs. 4.6% (passing). The Brazilian sit-rise test (Brito 2014, Eur J Prev Cardiol) similarly predicts mortality.

**Insider truth.** Most consumer mobility content — **Functional Range Conditioning (Andreo Spina)** and Kelly & Juliet Starrett's *Built to Move* (2023) — relies on practitioner experience and small case series rather than RCTs. Classical PT/strength research strongly supports loaded carries, hip mobility (90/90, deep squat hold), thoracic rotation drills, and single-leg work; FRC-style CARs are likely useful but unvalidated.

**Practical.** Build mobility into warm-ups (5–10 min) rather than standalone sessions: 90/90 hip switches, deep squat hold, thoracic open-books, dead-bugs, single-leg balance. At least one weekly heavy carry session (suitcase carries, farmer's walks) and 1–2 single-leg loaded movements (split squats, step-ups). Test annually: 10-second SLS, sit-rise test, deep-squat hold.

### 12. Walking, NEAT, and the death of the 10,000-step myth

**Core claim.** Daily steps confer steep mortality benefits up to ~7,500–8,000/day, with most benefit accrued well below the marketing-derived "10,000-step" target. **NEAT** (non-exercise activity thermogenesis) accounts for up to a 2,000 kcal/day inter-individual difference.

The "10,000 steps" number originated as a **1965 Yamasa Tokei pedometer marketing slogan ("Manpo-kei")** — never grounded in physiology. **Lee 2019 (JAMA Intern Med)** in 16,741 women (mean 72): vs. 2,700 steps/day, those at 4,400 had **41% lower mortality**; benefits plateaued at ~7,500. **Paluch 2022 (Lancet Public Health)** meta-analysis of 15 cohorts, n=47,471: adults <60 optimum ~8,000–10,000/day; ≥60 optimum ~6,000–8,000; mortality benefits begin from ~2,500–4,000. **Del Pozo Cruz 2022 (JAMA Intern Med)** in UK Biobank n=78,500: each additional 2,000 steps/day reduced all-cause, CV, and cancer mortality. Higher peak-30-min cadence (≥40 steps/min) added incremental benefit.

**James Levine (Mayo)** coined NEAT (Science 1999); in 1,000-kcal overfeeding, NEAT changes accounted for ~10× the variance in fat gain across subjects. Lean vs. obese subjects differed by ~150 min/day of standing/walking.

**Practical.** Target **7,500–9,000 steps/day** including a daily 20–30 min walk at brisk cadence (≥100 steps/min). NEAT levers: standing desk, walking meetings, parking far, stairs, post-meal 10-min walks (Buffey 2022 Sports Med — independently improve glycemic control). **Walking is daily life infrastructure, not part of the 3–5 hour exercise budget.**

### 13. Outdoor exercise and nature exposure

**Core claim.** Combining exercise with green/natural environments produces psychological and physiological benefits exceeding the same exercise indoors. ~120 minutes outdoors in nature per week associates with measurable health and well-being gains.

**Roger Ulrich 1984 (Science)** — cholecystectomy patients (n=46) with bedside views of trees had **shorter postoperative stays (7.96 vs. 8.70 days)**, fewer strong analgesics, fewer negative nurse evaluations than matched patients viewing a brick wall. The foundational paper of biophilic design research.

**Gregory Bratman 2015 (PNAS, Stanford → Univ. Washington)** — RCT, 38 healthy adults randomized to 90-min walk in nature vs. urban setting. **Nature walkers showed reduced rumination and decreased blood perfusion in the subgenual prefrontal cortex** (a region implicated in depression).

**White 2019 (Sci Rep)** in 19,806 English adults: **≥120 min/week in nature** → OR 1.59 for self-reported good health and OR 1.23 for high well-being; benefits peaked at 200–300 min/week, equivalent whether one long visit or several shorter ones.

**Qing Li (Nippon Medical School)** — forest bathing trips of 2–3 days produced ~50% increases in NK cell activity persisting >30 days, ~12% reductions in salivary cortisol, lower BP and sympathetic tone, increased parasympathetic activity. Effects partially reproduced by laboratory phytoncide inhalation.

**Insider truth.** The "20-5-3 rule" (Rachel Hopman, popularized in Michael Easter's *Comfort Crisis*) is a **synthesizing heuristic, not a tested protocol** — components are evidence-based, the specific numbers aren't. Vitamin D evidence is weaker than commonly portrayed: **VITAL trial (Manson 2019, NEJM, n=25,871)** found 2,000 IU/day for 5+ years did **not reduce invasive cancer or major CV events**. Sunlight remains valuable but largely as part of the broader outdoor package.

**Rucking** (15–30 lb pack, 30–60 min, 2–3×/wk) has emerged as an efficient hybrid: Zone 2 cardio + posterior-chain loading + outdoor exposure; preliminary research supports CV and bone-density benefits, though large civilian RCTs are pending.

**Practical.** ≥120 min/week outdoors, ideally ≥1 longer session (60–90 min) of hiking, trail running, or rucking. Phone away to maintain "soft fascination" cognitive restoration.

### 14. Social and partner exercise

**Core claim.** Exercising with a partner — especially a slightly stronger one, a romantic partner, or a small group — substantially improves adherence, work output, and retention. Mechanisms: indispensability, social comparison, accountability, and (for couples) self-expansion.

**Wallace, Raglin & Jastremski 1995 (J Sports Med Phys Fitness)** — the canonical "couples adherence" paper. 12-month dropout in a university fitness program: **6.3% in married pairs vs. 43% in married singles**.

**The Köhler effect** — originally described by Otto Köhler in 1926 with rowing teams. **Feltz, Kerr & Irwin 2011 (J Sport Exerc Psychol)**: plank-hold persistence with a virtually-present, slightly-superior partner was **24–200% longer** than solo. Effect strongest under conjunctive task conditions (group output = weakest member's output). Messé/Kerr follow-up: motivation gain is **largest with a moderately stronger partner (~40th-percentile-stronger)** and disappears if the gap is too large.

**Aron 2000 (J Pers Soc Psychol)** — couples randomly assigned to **novel/arousing shared activities** reported significantly increased relationship satisfaction. Self-expansion theory rationale for couples doing **novel** exercise together (climbing, hiking, dance, partner workouts).

**Burke 2006** meta-analysis: group-based interventions produced significantly greater PA outcomes than individual or true-control interventions (d ~0.5).

**Practical.** Schedule 1–2 weekly sessions with a partner, ideally one slightly fitter. Group classes, run clubs, regular gym partner. For couples, hiking and outdoor adventure hits the trifecta: cardio + nature + self-expansion-driven relationship benefits.

### 15. The 3–5 hour weekly budget — three templates

| Template | Strength | Zone 2 | VO2max | Mobility | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **3-hr minimum** | 2 × 45 min | 1 × 60 min | 1 × 30 min | warm-ups | 3 hr |
| **4-hr balanced** | 2 × 50 min | 2 × 50 min | 1 × 30 min | warm-ups + 10 min | 4 hr |
| **5-hr full** | 3 × 45–50 min | 2 × 60 min | 1 × 30 min | woven in | 5 hr |

**WHO 2020 guidelines** (Bull 2020, BJSM): 150–300 min moderate or 75–150 min vigorous aerobic + ≥2 days/week resistance covering all major muscle groups.

**Trade-offs.** If forced to cut: **drop Zone 2 volume before cutting strength** — sarcopenia is the larger midlife threat and Zone 2 can be partially substituted by daily brisk walking. **Never cut the VO2max session** — it produces disproportionate mortality benefit per minute. Mobility is warm-up/cool-down, not a standalone budget item. Walking is daily-life infrastructure, separate from the budget. **One outdoor session and one partner session per week leverage adherence multipliers without adding minutes.**

## Part III — The supporting pillars

### 16. Sleep as the foundation (revisited)

(See Part I, §5 for biology.) **Practical pillar.** **Hirshkowitz 2015 (NSF)** and **Watson 2015 (AASM/SRS joint consensus)** both used modified RAND/UCLA Appropriateness Method to land on 7–9 hours for adults 26–64; ≤6 hours = inappropriate. But **Windred 2024** showed regularity can dominate duration. The bedroom physiology requires a ~1°C core-temperature drop, supporting 60–67°F room temperature recommendations. Alcohol within 3 hours suppresses REM and fragments second-half sleep. Screen-before-bed effects are real but smaller than popularized; content arousal and ambient lux matter more than the device.

**Wearable use case:** trends in regularity, not stage analysis. Treat the "sleep score" as a behavioral nudge.

### 17. Nutrition without dogma

**Core claim.** Across rigorous trials, the diets that reliably reduce cardiometabolic risk and produce sustainable weight change share four features: (1) protein and fiber sufficient for satiety, (2) heavy weighting toward minimally processed foods, (3) Mediterranean pattern as the most replicated CV winner, (4) macronutrient ratio (low-fat vs. low-carb) is far less predictive than adherence and food quality.

**PREDIMED (Estruch 2013/2018, NEJM, n=7,447)** — Spanish RCT, 4.8-year median follow-up, stopped early. Mediterranean + EVOO HR 0.69; Mediterranean + nuts HR 0.72 — **~30% relative risk reduction** in major CV events vs. low-fat control. Caveat: 2018 retraction-republication corrected randomization issues at certain sites (household-level enrollment, clinic-level allocation); effect size preserved. **Lyon Diet Heart Study (de Lorgeril 1999, Circulation, n=605)** in secondary prevention showed even larger effects (HR ~0.28).

**Reynolds 2019 (Lancet)** — WHO-commissioned, 185 prospective studies (~135 million person-years) + 58 trials. **Per 8 g/day fiber: 5–27% reduction in CHD/T2D/colorectal cancer incidence and mortality.** Highest vs. lowest fiber: 15–30% lower all-cause and CV mortality. Most US adults consume ~15 g/day vs. 25–38 g/day target.

**Morton 2018 (BJSM)** — protein plateau at 1.62 g/kg/day for FFM. **PROT-AGE (Bauer 2013, JAMDA)** for adults >65: 1.0–1.2 g/kg baseline; ≥1.2 if exercising; 1.2–1.5 with chronic illness.

**DIETFITS (Gardner 2018, JAMA, n=609)** — healthy low-fat vs. healthy low-carb for 12 months: −5.3 kg vs. −6.0 kg, p=0.07 (NS). No interaction with predefined genotype or insulin secretion. **Macronutrient ratio is not destiny; quality and adherence are.**

**Hall 2019 (Cell Metab)** — NIH inpatient crossover, n=20 weight-stable adults, 2 weeks each on ultra-processed vs. unprocessed diet matched for sugar/fat/fiber/macros, ad libitum. **On UPF: ~500 kcal/day greater intake (p<0.0001), +0.9 kg weight gain.** First randomized causal evidence for ultra-processing as an independent overconsumption driver. Carlos Monteiro's **NOVA classification** is the framework.

**The GLP-1 reframing.** **STEP 1 (Wilding 2021, NEJM, n=1,961)** — semaglutide 2.4 mg: −14.9% body weight at 68 weeks vs. −2.4% placebo; 86% lost ≥5%, 50% lost ≥15%. **SURMOUNT-1 (Jastreboff 2022, NEJM, n=2,539)** — tirzepatide 15 mg: **22.5% weight loss at 72 weeks**; 63% achieved ≥20%. **SELECT (Lincoff 2023, NEJM)** — semaglutide reduced 3-point MACE by 20% (HR 0.80) in non-diabetic CVD patients — first hard CV outcome benefit for an obesity drug. These trials reframe the moral framing of "willpower" diets.

**Practical.** Mediterranean-style template (vegetables, legumes, fruit, whole grains, fish 2×/wk, EVOO, nuts, modest dairy, limited red/processed meat). Protein 1.2–1.6 g/kg/day, 0.4 g/kg/meal. Fiber ≥30 g/day. Reduce ultra-processed foods. Pharmacotherapy at BMI ≥30 (or ≥27 + comorbidity) with failed lifestyle is now legitimate.

### 18. Mental health and the U-curve of well-being

**Core claim.** Structured exercise produces moderate antidepressant effects comparable to first-line treatments; dietary quality independently improves mood; well-being follows a U-shaped curve that bottoms in the early-to-mid 40s.

**Schuch 2016 (J Psychiatr Res)** — 25 RCTs, pooled SMD ≈ 1.11 unadjusted, ~0.66 trim-and-fill corrected; resistance exercise SMD 0.99 across 33 RCTs (n=1,877). **Singh 2023 (BJSM)** umbrella review of 97 reviews, 1,039 trials, 128,119 participants: median SMD ≈ −0.43 for depressive symptoms; effects "comparable to or slightly greater than" psychotherapy and pharmacotherapy for mild-to-moderate symptoms. The viral "1.5× more effective than antidepressants" headline was an overinterpretation; honest read is "comparable, possibly slightly better."

**SMILES trial (Jacka 2017, BMC Med, n=67)** — 12 weeks, modified Mediterranean diet vs. social-support befriending in moderate-severe MDD. MADRS dropped 7.1 points more in diet group; **32.3% remission vs. 8.0%; NNT ≈ 4.1**. Caveats: small, criticized for blinding/recruitment (Molendijk 2018).

**Blanchflower 2021 (J Popul Econ)** — 145 countries, 444 significant country estimates with controls. **Average happiness nadir age 47.2 in advanced countries, 48.2 in developing.** Mirror unhappiness maximum age 49. David Blanchflower (Dartmouth) and Andrew Oswald (Warwick) have stood by the U-curve through Galambos critiques; 2023 update reaffirmed.

**Hill & Turiano 2014 (Psych Sci)** — MIDUS, n≈6,000, ~14-year follow-up. **Higher purpose → lower all-cause mortality (HR ~0.85 per SD)**, independent of positive affect, social relations, retirement. Effect held in 30s–40s.

**Practical.** PHQ-9 yearly. 3–5 sessions/week MVPA + 2 resistance for mood. Mediterranean pattern. Tele-CBT (BetterHelp, Talkspace) is non-inferior to in-person for depression/anxiety in 2022–2024 meta-analyses. **Anticipate the well-being trough as normal physiology of life-stage**, not personal failure.

### 19. Social connection as a longevity intervention

**Core claim.** Robust social ties predict lower mortality with effect sizes rivaling smoking cessation and exceeding obesity and inactivity. **Quality of midlife relationships outperforms cholesterol as a predictor of late-life health.**

**Holt-Lunstad 2010 (PLoS Med)** — 148 studies, 308,849 participants, 7.5-year mean follow-up. **OR 1.50 (1.42–1.59) for survival** with strong relationships — comparable to smoking ~15 cigarettes/day. Strongest for complex social-integration measures (OR 1.91), weakest for binary "lives alone" (OR 1.19). **Holt-Lunstad 2015 (Perspect Psychol Sci)** — 70 studies, 3.4 million participants: social isolation OR 1.29, loneliness OR 1.26, living alone OR 1.32. **Effect was *larger* in samples <65** — directly relevant to a 42-year-old.

**Berkman & Syme 1979 (Am J Epidemiol)** — Alameda County, n=6,928, 9-year follow-up. Most-isolated vs. most-connected: **men RR 2.3, women 2.8**, independent of baseline health, SES, behaviors. Foundational.

**Harvard Study of Adult Development.** Began 1938 with 268 Harvard sophomores + 456 Boston inner-city boys; now n=2,000+. **Robert Waldinger** (4th director, with Marc Schulz) summarized in *The Good Life* (2023): **relationship satisfaction at age 50 predicted physical health at 80 better than mid-life cholesterol**. Mechanism: stress regulation, behavior, mood.

**Surgeon General Vivek Murthy, May 2023 advisory "Our Epidemic of Loneliness and Isolation."** ~50% of US adults reported measurable loneliness pre-COVID. Lacking connection raises premature death risk equivalent to smoking up to 15 cigarettes/day; 29% increased heart disease risk, 32% stroke, 50% dementia. Six-pillar national strategy.

**Blue Zones caveat.** **Saul Justin Newman (UCL)** won the **2024 Ig Nobel in Demography** for showing supposed extreme-longevity pockets correlate with poor record-keeping and pension fraud (Italy 1997: 30,000 dead pensioners "alive"; Japan 2010: 230,000 missing centenarians; Costa Rica 2008: 42% of 99+ misstated age). Okinawa, the supposed sweet-potato region, has Japan's *lowest* vegetable intake and *highest* BMI. **The social-fabric observation may survive even where age claims don't.** Buettner's Power 9 are plausible heuristics, not established epidemiology.

**Practical.** Track relationships like cardio: 1–2 hours of in-person social time on most days. 3–5 close ties; isolation matters even when subjective loneliness is denied. Marriage/partnership *quality* > status. Workplace, faith group, sport league, volunteering = structural buffer (Murthy's "social infrastructure"). Don't outsource to apps.

### 20. Stress and HRV

**Core claim.** Chronic stress drives measurable physiological wear ("allostatic load") with cardiometabolic and neuropsychiatric consequences; HRV is a real biomarker but too noisy at the individual level for daily personal decisions; best-supported acute interventions are exercise, brief structured breathwork, nature exposure, and (modestly) mindfulness.

**Bruce McEwen** (Rockefeller, d. Jan 2, 2020) and Eliot Stellar (1993, Arch Intern Med) introduced **allostatic load** — cumulative cost of repeated/inefficient activation of stress-response systems. Operationalized as composite of cortisol, CRP, BP, waist-hip ratio, insulin resistance.

**Goyal 2014 (JAMA Intern Med)** — AHRQ-funded, 47 RCTs, n=3,515. Mindfulness meditation: **anxiety SMD 0.38 at 8 wk; depression 0.30; pain 0.33**. Low/insufficient evidence for positive mood, attention, sleep, weight, substance use. **No evidence meditation outperforms active controls** for any outcome. **The honest counterweight to wellness-influencer claims: real but modest, not superior to exercise or therapy.**

**Balban 2023 (Cell Rep Med)** — Huberman lab Stanford, n=111, 1 month of daily 5-min exercises. **Cyclic sighing (double inhale + long exhale) produced largest positive-affect gains (1.91 pts/day vs. 1.22 for mindfulness)** and greater reductions in respiratory rate. Caveats: small RCT, remote/self-report, short follow-up.

**Bratman 2015 (PNAS)** — 90-min nature walk reduced rumination and subgenual PFC activation.

**Insider truth on HRV.** Inter-individual variability is enormous; single-time-point measurements have poor reliability; wearables (Whoop, Oura, Apple) using PPG show acceptable agreement against ECG only in still, sleeping subjects. Useful only as **within-subject trend over weeks** — a 10–15% sustained drop from your own baseline = signal worth investigating (overtraining, illness, sleep debt). Absolute numbers between people are nearly meaningless.

**Practical.** HRV trends only (your 7-day rolling vs. 30-day baseline). For acute stress: 5 min cyclic sighing — cheapest, fastest evidence-based tool. Daily 10–20 min in green space; weekly 90+ min nature exposure. Mindfulness: useful, modest. **Most powerful stress intervention remains aerobic exercise + sleep regularity + social connection — all upstream of HRV.**

### 21. Alcohol: the J-curve is dead

**Core claim.** The decades-old J-curve (light drinking → lower mortality than abstainers) was largely driven by methodological confounders — most importantly the "sick quitter" contamination of the abstainer reference group. **There is no level of alcohol intake at which net health benefit is robustly demonstrated; cancer risk begins at very low intake.**

**Zhao, Stockwell et al. 2023 (JAMA Network Open)** — 107 cohorts, >4.8 million participants. After adjusting for study-quality biases (especially abstainer bias), **no statistically significant mortality reduction at any level**. Risk of all-cause mortality rises significantly above ~25 g/day (~2 drinks) for women and ~45 g/day (~3 drinks) for men.

**Biddinger 2022 (JAMA Network Open)** — UK Biobank, n=371,463. Mendelian randomization. Conventional analysis showed classic J/U shape; MR analysis showed monotonic risk increase — **no protective dose**. Confirmed by Lankester 2021 (PLOS One) and Rosoff 2020 (PLOS Med).

**Anderson et al. 2023 (Lancet Public Health)** — WHO/IARC formal position: alcohol is Group 1 carcinogen; "no safe level." **Canada's CCSA (Paradis 2023)** replaced 2011 guidelines (10–15 drinks/week) with: 0 = no risk; 1–2/week = low; 3–6/week = moderate; ≥7 = increasingly high. Never exceed 2 drinks/occasion.

**Surgeon General Murthy, January 3, 2025** — alcohol identified as the **third leading preventable cause of cancer in the US, after tobacco and obesity**: ~100,000 cancer cases and ~20,000 cancer deaths annually. Seven cancers with established causal links: **breast, colorectal, esophageal, liver, oral, pharyngeal, laryngeal**. Breast cancer risk rises at intakes as low as 1 drink/day. Called for cancer warning labels.

**The 2025–2030 Dietary Guidelines are unsettled.** Two parallel reports reached opposing conclusions: **NASEM (Dec 2024)** found moderate consumption associated with *lower* all-cause mortality and CVD vs. never-drinkers, but *higher* cancer risk; **ICCPUD (Jan 2025)** found rising mortality risk at any level. Political and industry pushback ensued. As of early 2026, existing 2020–2025 DGA limits remain operative pending the 2025–2030 release; expected direction is more cautionary.

**Practical.** **There is no longer an evidence-based recommendation to drink alcohol for health reasons.** If he doesn't drink, no reason to start. If he does, the evidence-aligned target is closer to Canada's CCSA (≤2 drinks/week) than US guidelines (≤14/week men). Avoid binges (≥5 drinks/occasion). No alcohol within 3 h of bed.

### 22. Preventive screening and labs at 42

**Annual basics.** CBC, CMP, lipid panel **+ apoB**, HbA1c, fasting glucose, TSH, BP, weight, waist circumference, PHQ-9, GAD-7. Skin self-exam + dermatology baseline if fair-skinned/family history melanoma. Dental q6mo; eye exam q1–2y; vision/hearing baseline.

**ApoB.** **NLA Expert Consensus 2024 (J Clin Lipidol)** recommends apoB measurement for all primary-prevention adults; in LDL-C/apoB discordance (common in metabolic syndrome), **apoB is more reliable**. Goals: <90 primary-prevention low-risk; <80 high-risk; <65 very high. The **2026 ACC/AHA/Multi-society Dyslipidemia Guideline** (Circulation 2026) formally elevated apoB and retired the 2018 cholesterol guideline.

**Lp(a).** **Koschinsky 2024 NLA update**: **measure once in every adult**. <75 nmol/L (30 mg/dL) low; ≥125 nmol/L (50 mg/dL) high. ~20% of population elevated. Cascade-screen first-degree relatives. Therapies in trials: olpasiran (OCEAN(a)-Outcomes, completing 2031, OCEAN(a)-DOSE phase 2 showed >95% reduction), pelacarsen (HORIZON, 2026 readout), lepodisiran (ALPACA phase 2: −93.9% at day 180), oral muvalaplin (~−65%).

**Glycemic.** ADA: HbA1c 5.7–6.4 = prediabetes; ≥6.5 = diabetes. Fasting insulin and HOMA-IR (>2.5 typically insulin-resistant) are not in formal guidelines but informative when HbA1c is normal but central adiposity is present.

**CAC scoring.** 2018 ACC/AHA Class IIa for asymptomatic adults 40–75 with LDL-C 70–189 at borderline (5–7.5%) or intermediate (7.5–<20%) 10-year ASCVD risk. CAC=0 → defer statin, recheck 5–10 yrs; 1–99 → favor statin if >55; ≥100 or ≥75th percentile → statin indicated. ~⅓ of intermediate-risk reclassified down with CAC=0 (Mortensen 2020 JAHA).

**Colorectal cancer screening.** **USPSTF May 18, 2021** — Grade B starting at **age 45** (lowered from 50). Options: colonoscopy q10y, FIT annually, FIT-DNA q1–3y, sigmoidoscopy q5y, CT colonography q5y. Driven by rising incidence in <50.

**Lung cancer screening.** **USPSTF March 9, 2021** — Grade B annual low-dose CT in adults 50–80 with ≥20 pack-year history, current or quit within 15 years.

**Once or rarely.** Lp(a) once; CAC if borderline-intermediate ASCVD risk or family history premature CAD (~age 40–45 first scan reasonable); HCV antibody once; HIV once.

**Vaccines.** Tdap q10y, annual flu, COVID per current rec, HPV through 45 if not done.

**Skip unless indicated.** **Whole-body MRI marketing (Prenuvo $2,499/scan, Ezra)** — USPSTF and major academic centers (Fred Hutch, UW Medicine, ACR) do **not** endorse for asymptomatic screening. Incidentaloma rates **16–36%** with downstream biopsy/imaging cost and anxiety, no demonstrated mortality benefit, and known limitations (no contrast, can't replace mammography/colonoscopy/low-dose chest CT, doesn't visualize coronaries). Same logic: indiscriminate CIMT, broad genomic profiling without family history, IgG food-sensitivity panels, hair mineral analysis, most "executive physical" add-ons.

**DEXA.** Informative but optional in 40s — body composition baseline (visceral fat, lean mass) and bone-density baseline before age-related loss accelerates; not USPSTF-recommended for men this age.

## Conclusion: the unhyped playbook

The hardest part of writing this book honestly is resisting the temptation to introduce novelty for its own sake. The evidence converges on a small number of interventions that compound: **lift heavy 2–3 times a week, accumulate Zone 2 cardio, do one VO2max session, walk a lot, sleep regularly, eat a Mediterranean-style diet with adequate protein and fiber, drink less than you used to think was fine, invest in your wife and friends, get apoB and Lp(a) tested once, and book a colonoscopy at 45.** None of this is glamorous. All of it works.

What's genuinely new in the last few years is mostly bad news for the supplement-stack worldview. The J-curve for alcohol is dead. Whole-body MRI marketing has outpaced its evidence. CGMs in healthy non-diabetics remain a hypothesis, not a therapy. HRV is a within-subject trend tool, not a number to optimize daily. Walker's specific sleep statistics need editing. The Blue Zones may be a record-keeping artifact. CPAP doesn't reduce hard CV outcomes in the trials we have. TRT in TRAVERSE was non-inferior on MACE but raised AF, PE, AKI, and (surprisingly) fracture signals.

What is genuinely new and good news: **GLP-1 agonists have become a serious tool for obesity-driven disease**, including OSA (SURMOUNT-OSA 2024) and CV events (SELECT 2023). **Lp(a)-lowering RNA therapies are in Phase 3** with first outcome readouts beginning 2026. **The 2024 Lancet Commission update raised the modifiable-dementia fraction to 45%**. **US POINTER 2025 replicated FINGER in a diverse American population.** **Sleep regularity (Windred 2024) is now a stronger mortality predictor than duration**, which changes the prescription from "8 hours" to "consistent timing."

The 42-year-old's defining advantage is that **almost everything that matters compounds with time**. A man who starts apoB management, resistance training, and sleep regularity at 42 is not a slightly improved version of a man who starts at 62 — he is a fundamentally different person at 70. The Mendelian-randomization data on apoB-years, the Type II fiber dynamics of sarcopenia, and the trajectory data from the Harvard Study of Adult Development all point to the same conclusion: **the boring interventions, started now, are the ones that buy decades**. The book's job is to help him pick a few and stick with them — together with his wife, outside when possible, without the supplement stack.
